# Evidence: Pick the moment to deliver an ask

## Sources

- Duflo, E., Kremer, M., & Robinson, J. (2011). Nudging farmers to use fertilizer: Theory and experimental evidence from Kenya. *American Economic Review*, 101(6), 2350–2390. https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.101.6.2350 Supports: making the offer while money is in hand, and attaching a deadline only where people put the action off. Farmers in western Kenya bought fertilizer in response to a small, time-limited discount (free delivery) offered just after harvest; the authors model farmers who postpone the purchase until the money is gone. The abstract of the working-paper version (https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w15131/w15131.pdf) adds that later discounts had a smaller impact. Abstracts only.
- Casaburi, L., & Willis, J. (2018). Time versus state in insurance: Experimental evidence from contract farming in Kenya. *American Economic Review*, 108(12), 3778–3813. https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20171526 Supports: asking whether the payment can move to when money arrives. Crop insurance whose premium was deducted from harvest revenue was taken up by 72% of farmers, against 5% for the same insurance paid up front; the gap was largest among poorer farmers. Abstract only.
- Mani, A., Mullainathan, S., Shafir, E., & Zhao, J. (2013). Poverty impedes cognitive function. *Science*, 341(6149), 976–980. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1238041 Supports: the attention question. The same farmers scored lower on tests of thinking before harvest, when short of money, than after it. Abstract only; that the farmers were sugarcane growers in India is from Princeton University's account of the study.
- Kaur, S., Mullainathan, S., Oh, S., & Schilbach, F. (2025). Do financial concerns make workers less productive? *The Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 140(1), 635–689. https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjae038 Supports: the attention question. Abstract only.
- The Behavioural Insights Team (2024). *EAST: Four simple ways to apply behavioural insights* (revised and updated edition). https://bi.team/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/BIT-EAST.pdf Supports: listing landmarks and life changes as candidate moments. Its "Make it timely" chapter says the same offer made at different times can have different effects, and that behavior may be easier to change when routines are disrupted, as on moving home or becoming a parent, or when people feel they are making a fresh start.
- Datta, S., & Mullainathan, S. (2012). *Behavioral Design: A New Approach to Development Policy*. CGD Policy Paper 016. https://www.cgdev.org/sites/default/files/Datta_Mullainathan_Behavioral_Design.pdf Supports: the money calendar. It proposes lining up the timing of demands such as school fees with the timing of income, above all for people with seasonal incomes.
- Dai, H., Milkman, K. L., & Riis, J. (2014). The fresh start effect: Temporal landmarks motivate aspirational behavior. *Management Science*, 60(10), 2563–2582. https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901 Supports: treating a landmark as a frame to test, not a finding. Three studies of existing records, with no experiment: searches for "diet", gym visits, and commitments to goals rise after the start of a week, month, year, or semester, a birthday, or a holiday. Abstract, and the description of the data.

## Where it has been tested

Searched November 2026.

| Source | Type | Countries | Finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Duflo, Kremer & Robinson (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.101.6.2350) | Randomized field experiment; abstracts only | Kenya | A small, time-limited offer just after harvest raised fertilizer purchase; later discounts did less |
| [Casaburi & Willis (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20171526) | Randomized field experiment; abstract only | Kenya | Insurance paid for at harvest: 72% take-up, against 5% when paid up front. Letting farmers commit to pay one month later raised demand by 21 percentage points |
| [Barrera-Osorio et al. (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1257/app.3.2.167) | Randomized field experiment; abstract only | Colombia | Holding back part of a monthly school transfer until re-enrollment time raised enrollment, with the same attendance as monthly payment. It helped the children most at risk of dropping out |
| [Mani et al. (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1238041) | Before-and-after comparison of the same farmers; abstract only | India | Lower scores on thinking tasks before harvest than after |
| [Kaur et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjae038) | Randomized field experiment; abstract only | India | Workers paid earlier made fewer costly mistakes and produced 7% more, most of all the workers shortest of money |
| [Carvalho, Meier & Wang (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20140481) | Randomized survey timing; abstract only | United States | Low-income households surveyed before payday did no worse on thinking tasks or decision quality than those surveyed after. They were more impatient about money |
| [Beshears et al. | [Njuguna et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1097/qad.0000000000003939) | Randomized trial, 9,143 people who had missed an HIV appointment; abstract only | South Africa | Texts sent around two public holidays: 13.4% returned to care against 11.9% with no text at the first holiday, and 6.7% against 5.4% at the second. | [Verplanken & Roy (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvp.2015.11.008) | Field experiment, 800 participants; abstract only | Not stated in the abstract | A program promoting sustainable habits worked better among people who had recently moved home; the window appeared to last three months. Behavior was self-reported |

None found: a systematic review or meta-analysis of when an offer is made; a field trial outside wealthy Western countries that tested a calendar landmark or a life change separately from sending a message at all. Searches covered the fresh-start effect and its replications, life changes and habits, offers timed to harvest or payday, and thinking before and after payday.

What depends on local conditions is the calendar itself: when money arrives, when the large bills fall, and which dates people treat as a beginning. None of it transfers from another country, crop, or employer.

## Replication

Timing an offer or a payment to when people hold cash, or to when a bill falls due, changed behavior in three field trials in Kenya and Colombia. The attention finding is contested: it appeared among Indian farmers and workers and did not appear around payday among low-income households in the United States. Calendar landmarks rest on records of what people do after such dates and on one field experiment in the United States; the South African trial could not show that texts around a holiday worked, or that mentioning a fresh start added anything.

## What to check locally

1. When money arrives for this group, from the field team or payment records, not from a national harvest calendar.
2. When the large expenses fall, and which of them coincide with the landmark the team likes.
3. Whether the action can be completed at the chosen moment: stock, opening hours, an agent present.
4. Whether putting things off is a confirmed barrier, before any deadline is attached.
5. Who holds no cash at the chosen moment, and whether the second moment reaches them.
