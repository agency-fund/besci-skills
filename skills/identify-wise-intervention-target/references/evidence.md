# Evidence: Find the question a wise intervention must answer

## Sources

- Walton, G. M., & Wilson, T. D. (2018). Wise interventions: Psychological remedies for social and personal problems. *Psychological Review*, 125(5), 617–655. https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000115 Supports: the whole method. The article's principles include altering a specific meaning; attending to the capacity present in the situation and in people; and timing an intervention early in a transition. It groups interventions by three needs behind meaning making: "the need to understand, the need for self-integrity, and the need to belong".
- Walton, G. M., Murphy, M. C., Logel, C., Yeager, D. S., Goyer, J. P., Brady, S. T., et al. (2023). Abstract only.
- Yeager, D. S., Hanselman, P., Walton, G. M., Murray, J. S., Crosnoe, R., Muller, C., et al. (2019). A national experiment reveals where a growth mindset improves achievement. *Nature*, 573, 364–369. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1466-y Supports: the same check. No review of wise interventions outside wealthy Western countries was found. The trials that exist are almost all of growth mindset in schools, and they disagree. Nothing was found on belonging interventions outside the United States. What depends on the place is whether the setting backs the message: an exam system with one attempt does not support "you can improve".

## Replication

Contested, and different for each kind of intervention. Belonging has a large multi-site trial behind it, in one country, with a stated condition. Growth mindset has many trials and a pooled effect near zero. Self-affirmation produced benefits in early school studies that a large replication in the same setting did not find.

## What to check locally

1. Whether the question makes sense in the local idiom; "belonging" and "ability" do not translate everywhere as single ideas.
2. Whether the setting gives a second attempt, feedback, or inclusion, as a fact and not a plan.
3. Whether people can do a written exercise, or need it spoken or in a group.
4. Whether anyone in the audience is in distress that an exercise about the self could worsen.
