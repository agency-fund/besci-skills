The scan did not look for trials of lottery rewards; the skill specifies a certain reward because that is what the trials above tested. What differs by place is what amount reads as fair, whether cash handed to a household is acceptable, whether paying health workers or officials is legal, and whether the service is reliable enough for a reward to have anything to work on.

## Replication

That a reward raises the rewarded action while it is paid has been found repeatedly, across regions and for one-off actions such as an immunization, an enrollment, or collecting a test result. Three things are less settled. What happens afterward: effects on health habits fade within months, and some immunization studies report uptake falling once payment stops. Whether payment displaces other motives: psychology experiments find it for simple tasks people already do willingly, one review found no sign of it for health behaviors, and several immunization studies report it. Whether a condition is needed: enforcement raised enrollment most across 35 studies, yet in Morocco a label did as well as a condition, and in Malawi unconditional transfers did more to delay pregnancy and marriage among girls who had dropped out of school.

## What to check locally

1. Whether the service is reliable: stock, staff, opening hours.
2. Whether someone other than the recipient can verify the behavior, and what one check costs.
3. A day's income and the cost of acting for this audience, from the field team.
4. Whether cash to households is acceptable, or goods are preferred.
5. Whether recipients include staff, officials, or volunteers, and whether paying them is legal.
6. Who the condition would exclude, and whether the program can accept that.
