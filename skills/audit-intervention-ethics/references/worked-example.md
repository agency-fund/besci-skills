# Worked example: a village sanitation pledge in East Java

A full run of the skill on a realistic case, showing the output it should produce. The case is illustrative, not a real program.

## The request

"We run a sanitation program in 30 villages in East Java with the district health office. Here is the draft. In each village our facilitators and the village head hold a meeting. Residents walk past the places where people defecate in the open, then see a village map on which each household without a latrine is marked with the household head's name. The village head sets a date by which the village will declare itself free of open defecation, and households sign a pledge sheet at the meeting. A children's group is formed to blow whistles at adults seen defecating in the open. The declaration is celebrated with a sign at the village entrance. Residents are told the village is choosing to end open defecation. They are not told that the named map will stay on the meeting-hall wall. The village head keeps the signed sheet, and also controls the lists for government aid. No trial is planned. Is this OK to run?"

## The output

```markdown
# Ethics review

**Intervention:** Program facilitators and the village head hold a meeting at which residents walk past open-defecation sites, see a map naming each household without a latrine, sign a pledge to end open defecation by a date the head sets, and form a children's group to whistle at adults seen in the open. It is meant to work by making the practice, and who still does it, visible to the whole village.
**Deliverer's hold over the audience:** The village head controls the lists for government aid. Children: enlisted as monitors. Dependents: renters and the poorest households rely on the head.

## Type
- **Transparent:** No. People can see the pledge and who is asking for it. They are not told that the named map stays on the wall or that children will watch them.
- **Avoidable:** No. It works through being seen by neighbors, which a household cannot decide against, and declining to sign happens in front of the head.
- **Rule for this type:** Not transparent, unavoidable: acceptable only if it serves people's interests, there is an easy route to object, its existence and purpose are clearly communicated, and nobody is held accountable for the result.
- **Conditions:** Serves their interests: met for health, not met for households that cannot afford a latrine. Easy route to object: not met. Clearly communicated: not met. Nobody held accountable: not met, because named households are held to a pledge in public.

## Checks

| Check | Rating | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Transparency | fail | The plain disclosure would be: "If you have no latrine, your name will be on a map in the hall, and children will whistle at you." The pledge and the date survive being said in advance. The map and the whistling rely on exposure nobody agreed to |
| Way out | fail | A household declines by not signing, in front of the head and its neighbors, with its name already on the map |
| Whose interest | concern | Less open defecation serves every household's health. The date is the head's, not the households' |
| Who bears the cost | fail | The poorest households, renters, and the elderly are the most likely to be named and the least able to build |
| Pressure | fail | Naming households and sending children to whistle at adults is shame by design |
| Responsibility and data | concern | Households are held in public to a date they did not set. The head keeps the signed sheet with no stated use or end date |

## Verdict: REVISE
Three of the rule's four conditions are unmet and four checks fail. All of it traces to the two parts that work by exposure: the named map and the children's monitoring. The walk, the pledge, the date, and the ceremony work by shared commitment and can stay. If the program keeps the named map or the children's role, the verdict is stop.

## Changes required before launch
1. Replace the named map with a count for the village, and keep any household list with the health worker.
2. Drop the children's whistling group. If monitoring stays, adult volunteers report counts, not names.
3. Tell residents before the meeting what will happen at it, who will hold any list, and for how long.
4. Say that signing is voluntary, let households sign in private after the meeting, and keep the signed sheet away from whoever controls the aid lists.
5. Before the date is set, agree how households that cannot afford a latrine will get one, and name a person outside the village to whom residents can object.

## Questions for an ethics board
- Whether children may take any part in the program, including the ceremony.

**Board review before launch:** required, because children are involved and the draft could cause distress.

## What this review could not assess
- Whether declining in view of the village head is safe in these villages. A local qualitative researcher can find out before the revised draft is reviewed again.
- How many households cannot afford a latrine, which sets the size of the cost problem. The district health office may know.
```
