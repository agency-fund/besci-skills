# Evidence: Write or revise research prose in a natural academic voice

## Sources

- Zezhen Wu's revision rules for research prose. Supports: the whole of `academic-voice.md`. The rules come from one researcher's practice of revising review and research manuscripts. The rules on structure (no staged sequences, no "not X but Y" pivots, varied paragraph shape, no closing summaries, one term for one construct) have no published test behind them.
- Kobak, D., González-Márquez, R., Horvát, E.-Á., & Lause, J. (2025). Delving into LLM-assisted writing in biomedical publications through excess vocabulary. *Science Advances*, 11(27), eadt3813. https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adt3813 Supports: the avoid-list. In more than 15 million PubMed abstracts from 2010 to 2024, "the appearance of LLMs led to an abrupt increase in the frequency of certain style words", and the authors estimate that "at least 13.5% of 2024 abstracts were processed with LLMs". Their published list of excess words includes, as style words, forms of delve, underscore, showcase, pivotal, crucial, intricate, intricacies, interplay, multifaceted, foster, garner, compelling, notably, ultimately, harness, unveil, elucidate, revolutionize, groundbreaking, transformative, utilize, facilitate, enhance, and highlight. It does not include tapestry, paradigm, vibrant, profound, poignant, arguably, importantly, fundamentally, inherently, undeniably, illuminate, or cutting-edge; those are on the avoid-list from practice. Read as the arXiv preprint (version 5) and the authors' word list at https://github.com/berenslab/llm-excess-vocab.
- Liang, W., Izzo, Z., Zhang, Y., Lepp, H., Cao, H., Zhao, X., Chen, L., Ye, H., Liu, S., Huang, Z., McFarland, D. A., & Zou, J. Y. (2024). Monitoring AI-modified content at scale: A case study on the impact of ChatGPT on AI conference peer reviews. *Proceedings of Machine Learning Research*, 235, 29575–29620. https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/liang24b.html Supports: the rule against inflated and promotional adjectives. "Between 6.5% and 16.9% of text submitted as peer reviews to these conferences could have been substantially modified by LLMs", and adjectives such as "commendable", "meticulous", and "intricate" became many times more likely. The paper's table of adjectives used disproportionately in AI-modified text includes pivotal, profound, and intricate. Read as the arXiv preprint.
- Oppenheimer, D. M. (2006). Consequences of erudite vernacular utilized irrespective of necessity: Problems with using long words needlessly. *Applied Cognitive Psychology*, 20(2), 139–156. https://doi.org/10.1002/acp.1178 Supports: "use, not utilize", and the plain word where it is as precise. Three experiments "find a negative relationship between complexity and judged intelligence", which "held regardless of the quality of the original essay". Abstract only.
- Braun, V., & Clarke, V. (2006). Using thematic analysis in psychology. *Qualitative Research in Psychology*, 3(2), 77–101. https://doi.org/10.1191/1478088706qp063oa Supports: first person for the authors' own analytic choices. The paper notes that writing in the first person is one way to acknowledge the analyst's active role. Full text.

## Where it has been tested

Searched November 2026. The skill is a procedure for writing, so this table records where its sources come from.

| Source | Type | Countries | Finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Kobak et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adt3813) | Corpus study; preprint and data read | Biomedical abstracts in English from authors worldwide; the estimated share differs by country and journal | Certain style words rose abruptly after language models appeared. The study shows these words mark machine-processed text; it does not test how readers judge it |
| [Liang et al. (2024)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/liang24b.html) | Corpus study; preprint read | Peer reviews at four AI conferences | A share of review text was probably modified by a language model, marked by a set of inflated adjectives |
| [Oppenheimer (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1002/acp.1178) | Experiments; abstract only | Not given in the abstract | Needless complexity lowered readers' judgment of the author |

What depends on local conditions is the convention, not the mechanism. Fields and languages differ on first person, on the passive, and on how much signposting a reader expects, and a journal's style guide overrides these rules.

## Replication

Not applicable. The rules are a practitioner's method. Each published source supports one part of the word list or one preference, and no study tests the rule set as a whole.

## What to check locally

1. The target journal's or funder's style guide, above all on first person, spelling, and headings.
2. Which words on the avoid-list are technical terms in the author's field.
3. Whether the author's co-authors accept the changes to any sentence that states a finding.
4. Whether the venue requires a statement on the use of AI in writing.
