The method:

- Wu, Z., Goldberg, B., & Xu, R. (2026, July 1). *Measuring agency in a digital context*. The Agency Fund. https://theagencyfund.substack.com/p/measuring-agency-in-a-digital-context Supports: the whole method. The post names three steps (define agency for the local problem; measure with the right tools, on the right data, at the right moment; test and validate the measures), separates on-platform data (in-app surveys, chatbot interactions, task behavior) from off-platform data (phone or in-person surveys, interviews, observation, records), defines reliability and construct, content, and criterion validity, and asks that a model's ratings be compared with interactions coded by trained reviewers. - Wu, Z., On, R., Walsh, J., Korley, E., Madon, T., & Wong, L. Its user-evaluation pages advise a brief self-report of three items or fewer, asked inside the chat right after a task; one trace measure combined with a short self-report; an outside check; and decoupled questions, because questions about the product carry halo effects. Its model-evaluation pages advise settling agreement among human raters first and trusting a model judge when its agreement with them is above 0.8, and warn that model judges may do poorly in low-resource languages. Those pages read in full.

No study was found that validates an agency, trust, or dependence measure delivered inside a chat on WhatsApp or a similar channel in a low-income country, or that tests this skill's pairing of on- and off-platform measures. The searches covered reviews of mobile and chatbot survey modes in low- and middle-income countries, cross-country tests of self-efficacy and locus of control scales, studies of decision-making questions, and studies of model coding in low-resource languages. What depends on the place is whether users read the items as intended: the words for confidence and control, who is expected to decide, whether a shared phone lets someone answer privately, and whether a model can read the language users write in.

## Replication

Mixed. Methods for pretesting a scale, checking its structure, and testing invariance are long established. Whether agency measures carry across countries is contested: a set of new scales held up across six African countries and one general self-efficacy scale kept one dimension across 25, but the same scale failed tests of equivalence in other studies, personality items failed in face-to-face surveys across 23 countries, and the way decision-making questions are built changed conclusions. Evidence on agency with respect to AI tools, and on a model as a measure in low-resource languages, comes from single studies and preprints.

## What to check locally

1. Whether users read each item as intended, in their language and on their channel: run cognitive interviews before fielding.
2. Whether users answer the tool's own questions differently from an enumerator's: compare a sample.
3. Whether one account is one person, and whether a shared phone lets the user answer privately.
4. Whether model codes hold up in this language and in each subgroup the plan compares.
5. Whether asking about decisions at home is safe here.
