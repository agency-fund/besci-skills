# Worked example: savings among Nairobi market vendors

A full run of the skill on a realistic case, showing the output it should produce. The case is illustrative, not a real program.

## The request

"Goal: increase long-term savings among informal-sector market vendors in Nairobi so they have a buffer against income shocks. What we know comes from one field visit to Gikomba market. The vendors we would reach are mostly women with fixed produce stalls. They count cash at the stall at the end of the day before heading home. Neighbors watch and sometimes ask to borrow. A husband often waits at home for the takings. Most are in a weekly chama. There is an M-Pesa agent kiosk in the market that they use every day, and the nearest bank is about 40 minutes away. We also plan to work in Toi market but have not been. Can you map the audience and context?"

## The output

```markdown
# Audience and context brief

**Program goal (as given):** Increase long-term savings among informal-sector market vendors in Nairobi so they have a buffer against income shocks.

## Population
- **As named:** "informal-sector market vendors in Nairobi"
- **Narrowed to:** Women who run fixed produce stalls in Gikomba market. Not hawkers who move through the market, and not yet vendors in Toi market, which the team has not visited.

## Decision moment
- **Where and when the choice is made:** At the stall at the end of the trading day, while counting the day's cash, before leaving for home. The cash is in hand. **Source:** seen by the team: one visit to Gikomba.
- **Who else is there or has a say:** Neighboring vendors watch the count and sometimes ask to borrow. A husband often waits at home for the takings. **Source:** seen by the team (neighbors); told by someone who lives it: vendors, on the same visit (husband).

## Channels that reach them today
- Weekly chama, a savings group whose members contribute in turn: most vendors belong; meets weekly. **Source:** told by someone who lives it: vendors, on the same visit.
- M-Pesa agent kiosk inside the market: used every day. **Source:** seen by the team: one visit to Gikomba.

## Constraints at the decision moment
- The count happens in view of other vendors, so money set aside is visible and can be asked for. **Source:** seen by the team: one visit to Gikomba.
- The nearest bank branch is about 40 minutes away, and the vendor is at the stall during trading hours. **Source:** told by someone who lives it: vendors, on the same visit; not timed by the team.

## Assumptions carried over from wealthy Western settings
- **Infrastructure:** "Savings" in the goal suggests an individual account at a bank. **Confirmed locally?** No. The bank is 40 minutes away and the mobile-money kiosk is in the market. **What to check:** where vendors keep money they do not spend today, and whether any of it is in a bank.
- **Who decides:** The goal treats the vendor as the person who decides what happens to the day's takings. **Confirmed locally?** Unknown. A husband often waits for the takings. **What to check:** ask vendors, away from the stall, who decides how much is kept back and whether money can be set aside before going home.

## Open questions
- Nothing here is confirmed for Toi market. Visit before treating the two markets as one group.
- Everything rests on one visit to Gikomba. Ask ten more vendors on another day whether the end-of-day count is where they decide what to keep.
- How many vendors hand the takings to a husband or another relative is not known. "Often" is the team's impression from one visit.
- Whether vendors already hold a balance in mobile money overnight is not known. Ask at the kiosk and ask vendors.
```
