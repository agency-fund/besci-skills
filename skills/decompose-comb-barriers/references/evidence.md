# Evidence: Find out why people are not doing a behavior

## Sources

- Michie, S., van Stralen, M. M., & West, R. (2011). The behaviour change wheel: A new method for characterising and designing behaviour change interventions. *Implementation Science*, 6, 42. https://doi.org/10.1186/1748-5908-6-42 Supports: the model. Capability is "the individual's psychological and physical capacity to engage in the activity concerned"; opportunity is "all the factors that lie outside the individual that make the behaviour possible or prompt it"; motivation is "all those brain processes that energize and direct behaviour", including habit and emotion. The paper divides capability into physical and psychological, opportunity into physical and social, and motivation into reflective and automatic. It also says that physical capability is built by training and that opportunity is changed through the environment, which is why the persistence test matters. Full text; the sections on the model and its reliability were read.
- Michie, S., Atkins, L., & West, R. (2014). *The Behaviour Change Wheel: A Guide to Designing Interventions*. Silverback Publishing. The authors' fuller guide to the method. The book is not open, and nothing in this skill rests on it alone.
- Kettle, S., & Persian, R. (2022). *Target, Explore, Solution, Trial, Scale: An introduction to running simple behavioural insights projects*. The Behavioural Insights Team. https://www.bi.team/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/BIT-Handbook-How-to-run-simple-BI-projects.pdf Supports: the place of the task. Its Explore stage has a step "Use behavioural science frameworks to categorise barriers", which uses COM-B and comes before any solution is designed. That step was read in full.
- Schmidtke, K. A., & Drinkwater, K. G. (2021). A cross-sectional survey assessing the influence of theoretically informed behavioural factors on hand hygiene across seven countries during the COVID-19 pandemic. *BMC Public Health*, 21, 1432. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-021-11491-4 Supports: ranking from local evidence. Abstract only.
- Willmott, T. J., Pang, B., & Rundle-Thiele, S. (2021). Capability, opportunity, and motivation: an across contexts empirical examination of the COM-B model. *BMC Public Health*, 21, 1014. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-021-11019-w Supports: the statement that the model has been tested as a predictor of behavior. Abstract only, with the country taken from the full text.

Three rules are this skill's own and come from no published source: the persistence test, the "discriminates because" line, and the ranking on evidence so far and cost of checking.

No review was found of how well COM-B fits behavior outside wealthy Western countries, and no study was found that tests whether a diagnosis made with COM-B leads to a better intervention than one made without it. Searches covered systematic reviews of COM-B and the Behaviour Change Wheel in low- and middle-income countries, critiques of the model's fit in non-Western settings, and surveys that test the model as a predictor of behavior.

The three components are general. Which one limits a behavior is local, and social opportunity varies most: who may do what, in front of whom, and who must agree.

## Replication

COM-B is a way of sorting influences on behavior, so there is no effect to replicate. The original paper reports agreement between two coders on policy documents and calls for research on whether the framework leads to more effective interventions. The two surveys above that measured the components found different patterns for different behaviors and countries, which is what a diagnostic model should expect and is the reason a diagnosis cannot be borrowed.

## What to check locally

1. Who else has a say in the behavior (spouse, in-laws, employer, group leader), asked of members of the audience.
2. Whether the materials, money, time, and services the behavior needs are present at the moment it should happen.
3. Whether each question can be asked in the local language without suggesting its answer.
4. Whether anyone on the team has watched the behavior, or its absence, at the place and time it occurs.
