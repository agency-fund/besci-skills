# Worked example: first-year students from rural districts at a public university in Tanzania

A full run of the skill on a realistic case, showing the output it should produce. The case is illustrative, not a real program.

## The request

"Behavior: first-year students from rural districts register for the second semester by the deadline. They arrive in October; the first continuous-assessment marks come back in week six. We interviewed 30 first-years from rural districts last year: 19 said some version of 'students from the villages do not finish here; the town students already know how the university works', most of them after the week-six marks. The student union pairs every rural first-year with a third-year mentor from a rural district. Hostel rooms are allocated by faculty, not by home region. The dean of students has had no harassment complaint by home region in two years, and none came up in the interviews; we have not asked the mentors. Our field officer has interviewed ten third- and fourth-years from rural districts, in Swahili, with written consent. Nine described a moment when they thought the university was not for people like them, and all ten said it passed during the first year. Notes from four:

- Neema, fourth year: missed a registration deadline in her first month because she did not know about the online portal. Thought 'a town student would have known; people like me are not meant for here.' Her mentor showed her the portal in week eight. Later found that everyone from her district had missed something that month.
- Daudi, third year: two week-six marks below the class average. Decided to go home after the semester. Then learned that a room-mate from Dar es Salaam had failed a test too. By the second semester he was around the average.
- Rehema, fourth year: did not speak in class for two months because of her accent. Thought the lecturers had decided about her. Asked to present in week ten, and the class listened. The three other women from her region had been silent too.
- Juma, third year: nearly left in week seven after a failed assessment. Talked to his mentor. It had passed by the end of the semester. As a mentor he hears first-years say what he thought.

We plan one 90-minute session in week two, led by two mentors, in Swahili, with a printed set read aloud because some students read slowly. We have registration records, and a sister campus that will not run the session this year."

## The output

```markdown
# Belonging story set

**Behavior:** First-year students from rural districts register for the second semester by the deadline.
**Transition:** Arrival in October; the first continuous-assessment marks come back in week six.
**The question newcomers are asking:** "Students from the villages do not finish here; the town students already know how the university works" (19 of 30 first-years from rural districts; interviews last year)
**Group:** First-year students from rural districts.

## What the setting offers (gate)
- **Who includes them:** A third-year mentor from a rural district for every rural first-year (student union); hostel rooms allocated by faculty, so rural and town students share (university rule).
- **Harassment or exclusion:** None reported: no complaint by home region to the dean of students in two years, and none raised in 30 interviews. The mentors have not been asked.
- **Verdict:** Delivers. One open point: ask the mentors whether rural first-years are mocked in the hostels.

## Collection
- **Tellers:** Ten third- and fourth-year students from rural districts, already interviewed, with written consent. Each approves the edited story before it is printed.
- **Questions:** What was hard in your first weeks here? What did you think it meant about you, or about students like you? What changed, and when?
- **Who interviews, when:** The field officer, in Swahili; done.

## Stories (working copy; final wording in the tellers' language)
### "Neema", fourth year
In my first month I missed a registration deadline. I did not know there was an online portal. I thought: a town student would have known; people like me are not meant for here. In week eight my mentor showed me the portal in ten minutes. Later I asked the others from my district. Every one of them had missed something that month. [Shortened; order kept.]
### "Daudi", third year
Two of my week-six marks were below the class average. I decided I would go home after the semester. Then I learned that my room-mate from Dar es Salaam had failed a test too, which I had not expected. By the second semester my marks were around the average. [Shortened.]
### "Rehema", fourth year
For two months I did not speak in class because of my accent. I thought the lecturers had already decided about me. In week ten one of them asked me to present, and the class listened. The three other women from my region told me they had been silent too. [Shortened.]
### "Juma", third year
I nearly left in week seven, after I failed an assessment. I talked to my mentor. By the end of the semester it had passed. Now I am a mentor, and the first-years tell me exactly what I thought. [Shortened; the teller asked that the course not be named.]

## Framing line
"We asked ten students from rural districts who are now in their third and fourth years what their first weeks here were like. Nine described a moment when they thought the university was not for people like them. All ten said it passed during the first year."

## Advice task
"Next October a student from a district like yours will arrive here and, around week six, think the university is not for people like them. First write a few lines on which of these stories is closest to your own first weeks. Then write that student a letter: what you felt, what it turned out to mean, and two things they should do. We will give your letter, without your name, to next year's first-years." Students who prefer to speak record the letter with a mentor.

## Delivery
- **When:** Once, in week two, before the week-six marks.
- **Form and run by:** A 90-minute session led by two third-year mentors; the printed set is read aloud in Swahili, and letters are written or recorded in the last 25 minutes.

## How you will know
- **Outcome:** Registration for the second semester by the deadline, from registration records.
- **Judged on:** First-years from rural districts on this campus against those on the sister campus, same intake.
- **Check on delivery only:** One item, "students like me belong at this university", asked in week eight.

## The record
The trials are from colleges in the United States. Among 26,911 students at 22 institutions, the exercise raised the rate of completing the first year full-time only where students' groups had real opportunities to belong (Walton et al., 2023). At one university, 86% of minority and first-generation students stayed enrolled through the second year, against 76% without it (Murphy et al., 2020). No field trial in a low- or middle-income country was found, so this is a first test here. No effect size is promised.

## Pre-test before use
Eight incoming first-years from rural districts and two mentors read the Swahili set. Pass: the first-years recognize at least two of the difficulties as ones they expect or have met, the mentors find no story that promises something the campus does not offer, and all eight can say what the letter asks for.

## What to check before relying on this
- "No harassment" rests on complaints and on first-year interviews. Ask the mentors about the hostels before week two.
- Rehema's story is about accent and speaking in class. Check with the pre-test group that rural first-years here recognize it.
```
