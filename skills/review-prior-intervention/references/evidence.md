# Evidence: Review a past program: bad idea or bad delivery?

## Sources

- Moore, G. F., Audrey, S., Barker, M., Bond, L., Bonell, C., Hardeman, W., Moore, L., O'Cathain, A., Tinati, T., Wight, D., & Baird, J. (2015). Process evaluation of complex interventions: Medical Research Council guidance. *BMJ*, 350, h1258. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.h1258 Supports: the three themes of process evaluation, implementation (fidelity, dose, reach, and adaptations), mechanisms of impact, and context; the statement that "an intervention may have limited effects either because of weaknesses in its design or because it is not properly implemented"; and the caution that there is no consensus on how to divide implementation into fidelity, dose, and reach. Full text.
- Durlak, J. A., & DuPre, E. P. (2008). Implementation matters: A review of research on the influence of implementation on program outcomes and the factors affecting implementation. *American Journal of Community Psychology*, 41(3–4), 327–350. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10464-008-9165-0 Supports: checking delivery before judging the idea. Results from over 500 quantitative studies gave "strong empirical support to the conclusion that the level of implementation affects the outcomes obtained in promotion and prevention programs", and 81 further reports identified at least 23 contextual factors that influence implementation. Abstract only.
- Kettle, S., & Persian, R. (2022). *Target, Explore, Solution, Trial, Scale: An introduction to running simple behavioural insights projects*. The Behavioural Insights Team. https://www.bi.team/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/BIT-Handbook-How-to-run-simple-BI-projects.pdf Supports: "Plan next steps if the intervention didn't work": follow-up research asking participants "whether the intervention actually reached them, what they thought about it, and whether/how it had an impact on their behaviour", with the warning that they "might tell you what they think you want to hear"; the advice not to retest with a larger sample when the estimate is precise or follow-up found a problem with the intervention; and the four next steps (the same intervention with a new population or larger sample, an improved design, a new intervention for the same behavior, a new target behavior). Full text.
- Skivington, K., Matthews, L., Simpson, S. A., et al. (2021). A new framework for developing and evaluating complex interventions: update of Medical Research Council guidance. *BMJ*, 374, n2061. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.n2061 Supports: that process evaluation "can help determine why an intervention fails unexpectedly", through questions on fidelity and quality of implementation, mechanisms of change, and context. Full text.
- The TOPS Program (2017). *Designing for Behavior Change: A Practical Field Guide*. https://www.fsnnetwork.org/resource/designing-behavior-change-practical-field-guide Supports: the split within idea failure. If the audience is reached and quality activities are delivered "but the Behavior is not changing, program planners should first reassess the appropriateness of the Activities selected, then reassess Bridges to Activities through Barrier Analysis". Full text.
- Mann, C., Shaw, A. R. G., Guthrie, B., Wye, L., Man, M.-S., Chaplin, K., & Salisbury, C. (2019). Can implementation failure or intervention failure explain the result of the 3D multimorbidity trial in general practice: mixed-methods process evaluation. *BMJ Open*, 9(11), e031438. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2019-031438 Supports: the "both" classification. The process evaluation concluded that implementation failure contributed to the trial's null result, "but so did intervention failure". Abstract only.

The causal claim check, the confirmed-or-inferred label on every finding, and the rule that an approximate figure can decide its own dimension are Joe Speed's method and come from no source.

The procedure does not depend on the population. The record does: whether delivery was logged by carrier, site, or worker, and whether anyone asked participants what reached them, decides how much the review can say.

## Replication

Not applicable. No source tests whether two reviewers give the same classification to the same record.

## What to check locally

1. Who kept the delivery logs, and whether anything independent of the people delivering backs them.
2. Whether any participant was asked what reached them and what they did with it.
3. Whether the outcome came from a comparison with a control group, and how wide its confidence interval is.
4. Whether anything changed around the program during the period: staff, supply, prices, a competing program.
