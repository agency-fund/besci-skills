# Worked example: a return-date card for antenatal visits in northern Ghana

A full run of the skill on a realistic case, showing the output it should produce. The case is illustrative, not a real program.

## The request

"We work with two health centers outside Tamale. Behavior: pregnant women who come for a first antenatal visit come back on the date the midwife gives them. About half miss it. From 30 interviews, the barrier is that women leave without knowing the date: the midwife writes it in English in the maternal record book, most of the women did not go to school, and the person who pays for transport, usually the husband or his mother, never hears the date. Many women count time in market days; the local market meets every sixth day.

We have drafted a return-date card for the midwife to fill in and the woman to take home. Version A shows a one-month calendar with the date circled. Version B shows a row of market stalls, one per market day, with the midwife crossing off how many market days to wait. Both have a picture of a woman handing the card to a man. They are printed mock-ups. Our designer made them and is keen to run the sessions. We can reach about ten women per clinic day, the family members who come with them, and all four midwives. Our field officer speaks Dagbani and did not work on the design. The pilot starts in six weeks. Can you plan how we test the card first?"

# Trying the return-date card with a few people before the pilot

**Draft under test:** A return-date card filled in by the midwife and taken home by the woman, in two versions: A, a one-month calendar with the date circled; B, a row of market stalls with the market days to wait crossed off. Version shown: printed mock-up.
**What it must do:** Pregnant women who attend a first antenatal visit return on the date given. The card removes one barrier: the woman, and the person who pays for transport, do not know the date.
**What the team will decide with it:** Which version, if either, goes into the pilot in six weeks.

## Learning questions
| # | Question | Tied to | What will be observed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | After the midwife fills in the card and explains it once, can the woman say when to come back? | Barrier: the woman does not know the date | Ten minutes later she states the day or the count of market days, and it matches what the midwife set |
| 2 | Can a family member who was not in the room work out the date from the card alone? | Barrier: the person who pays for transport never hears the date | Handed the card with no explanation, the person says what it asks and when |
| 3 | Which version do women who did not go to school read correctly more often? | Step of use: reading the card at home | Correct answers to question 1, counted by version |
| 4 | Can a midwife fill in each version correctly during a normal visit? | Step of use: filling in the card | In a role play with a set date, the card is correct and the time taken is noted |
| 5 | Did women who missed a visit know their date? | Barrier: checks that not knowing the date is what stops them | Women describe, in their own words, why the last missed visit was missed |

## Who and how many
| Group | Why they may react differently | People per round | How they are found |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pregnant women who did not go to school | Cannot read the English date; may count in market days | 5 | Asked at the antenatal clinic day, in order of arrival |
| Pregnant women with some schooling | May prefer the calendar | 5 | Same |
| Husbands or mothers-in-law | See the card without the midwife's explanation; pay for transport | 5 | Those accompanying women on the clinic day |
| Midwives | Fill in the card under time pressure | 4 (all of them) | Both health centers |

- **Rounds:** Two, one week apart. Between rounds the cards are changed only where the decision rule says so. Each woman sees one version; versions alternate.
- **Not reached:** Women who never come for a first visit, and family members who do not come to the clinic. A husband who stays home may read the card differently from one who comes along.

## Method
- **Method:** Use while observed, for women and family members. Role play, for midwives.
- **Why this one:** Questions 1 to 4 are about what people can do with the card, which has to be watched; a woman asked "is it clear?" will say yes.
- **Setting:** A bench outside the consulting room, after the visit; about 15 minutes per person. The midwife fills in the mock-up with a practice date, not the woman's real one, and the real date is given in the usual way afterward.
- **Run by:** The field officer, in Dagbani, with a second staff member taking notes. The designer does not attend the sessions.

## Session guide
1. Opening: "We are trying out a card to see whether it is any use. We are testing the card, not you. If something is confusing, that is the card's fault and it helps us to hear it." Ask for consent. Do not say who made the card.
2. Task for women: the midwife fills in the card and explains it once. The tester then talks with the woman about her journey to the clinic for ten minutes (question 5 is asked here), then says: "Show me on the card when you come back."
3. The note-taker watches where she points, whether she counts stalls or days aloud, whether she turns to someone for help, and what she says.
4. Tell back: "In your own words, what is this card asking you to do?" Then: "What will you do with it when you get home?"
5. Task for family members: hand over a filled-in card with no explanation. "Someone in your house brought this home. What does it tell you?"
6. Role play for midwives: "The next visit is in four weeks. Fill in the card as you would in a visit." Time it. Then: "What would stop you doing this for every woman?"
7. Closing: "What would you change?" Thank the person. Make sure the woman has her real return date.

## Decision rule (fixed before the first session)
| Learning question | Keep if | Change and retest if | Drop if |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | At least 4 of 5 women in each group give the right date | 2 or more make the same mistake | After one round of changes, fewer than 3 of 5 women with no schooling give the right date |
| 2 | At least 4 of 5 family members give the right date | 2 or more misread it the same way | After one round of changes, fewer than 3 of 5 do |
| 3 | One version has more correct answers among women with no schooling: take that version forward | The versions tie: keep both for round two | Not applicable |
| 4 | All 4 midwives fill it in correctly | Any midwife makes an error, or says it takes too long for a normal visit | After changes, 2 or more still make errors |
| 5 | Most women who missed a visit say they did not know the date | Not applicable | Most say they knew the date and missed for another reason: stop, and take the finding back to the barrier diagnosis |

- **Stop at once if:** Any woman leaves believing the practice date is her real return date, or says that being seen with the card at home would cause her trouble.
- **Stop testing when:** Round two turns up nothing the rule says to change. If it does, run a third round on that change only; the pilot date allows one.

## What this test cannot show
- Whether women with the card come back on time. That is for the pilot and the evaluation.
- What share of women in the district can read either version. These are counts from about ten women a round.
- Whether the card survives a month in a household: whether it is kept, lost, or shown to the person who pays for transport.

## What to check before relying on this
- Whether women tested at the clinic, minutes after the midwife's explanation, are a fair stand-in for a woman reading the card at home three weeks later. If the team can visit five women at home a week after round one, add that.
- Whether the market-day count holds across both catchment areas; a second market on a different cycle would break version B.

## Where you could go from here
That's the plan.
- Do a dry run of the session guide with the field officer and note-taker on two colleagues: worth it before the first clinic day, since the order (ten minutes of talk before "show me") is easy to get wrong and question 1 depends on it.
- Plan the pilot with `design-pilot-feasibility-study`: fits once round two settles on a version, because whether midwives can fill in a card for every woman on a busy day is something four role plays only touch on.
- Go back to the barrier with `decompose-comb-barriers`: fits only if question 5 finds that most women knew their date and missed for another reason.
I'd lean toward the dry run first, because a session run in the wrong order costs a clinic day you cannot get back.
Questions you could ask next: "How should the note-taker record answers given in Dagbani?" · "What do we do if both versions fail the rule in round one?"
