# Evidence: Describe an intervention so others can replicate it

## Sources

The task is a named step in these guides:

- Skivington, K., Matthews, L., Simpson, S. A., Craig, P., Baird, J., Blazeby, J. M., et al. (2021). A new framework for developing and evaluating complex interventions: update of Medical Research Council guidance. *BMJ*, 374, n2061. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.n2061 Supports: naming the parts and what each does. Programme theory "articulates the key components of the intervention and how they interact, the mechanisms of the intervention". In the ASSIST example, implementation "included an intervention manual that identified critical components and other components that could be adapted or dropped to allow flexible implementation while achieving delivery of the key mechanisms of change; and a training manual for the trainers". Read in full.
- OECD (2019). *Tools and Ethics for Applied Behavioural Insights: The BASIC Toolkit*. https://doi.org/10.1787/9ea76a8f-en Supports: writing for the people who will maintain the program, and naming the details a newcomer would drop. "Plans for implementing and scaling should, therefore, include instructions for the proper maintenance – physical or systemic – of the policy." In a Norwegian example, sales of energy-efficient appliances fell back "as new staff were not being trained in the role intended for the showing of lifetime costs". Practitioners should "produce material and instructions that fit these audiences and the situations in which this material is to be used". The section on implementation read.
- World Health Organization & ExpandNet (2011). *Beginning with the end in mind: Planning pilot projects and other programmatic research for successful scaling up*. https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241502320 Supports: the delivery system and the record of changes. Recommendation 8 is to "assess and document the process of implementation", including "required human resources and worker skills" and "supervision, incentive structures, costs, financing, logistics and the functioning of the management information system"; teams should also "document the changes in the package of interventions that are being made and the reasons why". Read in full.
- Walton, G. M., & Wilson, T. D. (2018). Wise interventions: Psychological remedies for social and personal problems. *Psychological Review*, 125(5), 617–655. https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000115 Supports: writing exact wording and who passes it on. Such interventions "depend for their effectiveness on details of their implementation and the nature of the context", and "errors can also arise when a complex idea is transmitted from person to person". Read in full.

The method:

- Hoffmann, T. C., Glasziou, P. P., Boutron, I., Milne, R., Perera, R., Moher, D., Altman, D. G., Barbour, V., Macdonald, H., Johnston, M., Lamb, S. E., Dixon-Woods, M., McCulloch, P., Wyatt, J. C., Chan, A.-W., & Michie, S. (2014). Better reporting of interventions: template for intervention description and replication (TIDieR) checklist and guide. *BMJ*, 348, g1687. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.g1687 Supports: the twelve items. The checklist asks for materials "including those provided to participants or used in intervention delivery or in training of intervention providers", with "where the materials can be accessed"; tailoring "if the intervention was planned to be personalised, titrated or adapted"; modifications: "If the intervention was modified during the course of the study, describe the changes (what, why, when, and how)"; and how well, planned and actual. For a protocol, items 10 and 12 "cannot be described until the study is complete". TIDieR covers "the intervention elements (and where relevant, comparison elements)", and although its emphasis is on trials, "the guidance is intended to apply across all evaluative study designs". Abstract and the checklist (https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/tidier/) read; the explanation for each item not read.
- Michie, S., Richardson, M., Johnston, M., Abraham, C., Francis, J., Hardeman, W., Eccles, M. P., Cane, J., & Wood, C. E. (2013). The behavior change technique taxonomy (v1) of 93 hierarchically clustered techniques: Building an international consensus for the reporting of behavior change interventions. *Annals of Behavioral Medicine*, 46(1), 81–95. https://doi.org/10.1007/s12160-013-9486-6 Supports: naming active content as techniques, and no code from memory. The taxonomy has 93 techniques in 16 groups. Six researchers coded 85 intervention descriptions; of the 26 techniques that occurred at least five times, 23 reached adjusted kappas of 0.60 or above. Abstract only.
- Campbell, M., Katikireddi, S. V., Hoffmann, T., Armstrong, R., Waters, E., & Craig, P. (2018). TIDieR-PHP: a reporting guideline for population health and policy interventions. *BMJ*, 361, k1079. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.k1079 Supports: the delivery-system section. Many population health and policy interventions "have characteristics that are important for their implementation and success but are not adequately captured in the original" checklist. Abstract only.
- Grant, S., Mayo-Wilson, E., Montgomery, P., Macdonald, G., Michie, S., Hopewell, S., & Moher, D. (2018). CONSORT-SPI 2018 Explanation and Elaboration: guidance for reporting social and psychological intervention trials. *Trials*, 19, 406. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13063-018-2735-z Supports: the specialist point for trials. It extends nine CONSORT items and adds one on stakeholder involvement. Abstract only.

Three rules are this skill's own and come from no source: a source tag on every item, with "recollection" as an allowed and labeled source; "available on request" counts as missing; and the rebuild check that closes every run.

## Where it has been tested

Searched November 2026. The skill is a reporting procedure and makes no claim about how people behave, so this table records where its sources come from and how often interventions are described well.

The procedure does not depend on the population. What a reader elsewhere needs most does: the language of the materials, who delivered them and with what supervision, and what the channel costs participants are the details a team takes for granted and a team in another place cannot guess.

## Replication

Not applicable; these are consensus reporting standards. The reviews above agree that most published interventions are described incompletely, with materials the gap most often found.

## What to check locally

1. Whether a written design exists, or the description will rest on recollection.
2. Whether the materials, in their final delivery languages, are in one place a reader can reach.
3. Whether the context a reader elsewhere would assume away is written into item 7: opening hours, who the messenger was, what the channel costs participants.
4. Whether a record of changes was kept during delivery, or modifications must be reconstructed.
5. Whether monitoring data exist for each essential part, or item 12 will say "not measured".
