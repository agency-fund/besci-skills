# Evidence: Ask people to plan when and where

## Sources

- Sheeran, P., Listrom, O., & Gollwitzer, P. M. (2025). The when and how of planning: Meta-analysis of the scope and components of implementation intentions in 642 tests. *European Review of Social Psychology*, 36(1), 162–194. https://doi.org/10.1080/10463283.2024.2334563 Supports: the form of the prompt, and the warning that field effects are small. Effects are given as d, the difference between groups in standard deviations. For behavior the effect was d = 0.27. It was larger for if-then plans than for plans that only schedule an action (0.43 against 0.29), for a time and a place than for a time alone (0.46 against 0.25), and when the plan was rehearsed (0.45 against 0.33). It was smaller when people added how, or for how long, they would act (0.24), and when the plan was recorded (0.31 against 0.44). Effects were stronger in the lab than in the field and when people were already motivated. The authors find substantial publication bias; an analysis that models it puts the average effect at d = 0.15 (95% CI 0.08 to 0.22). Read in part, from the authors' accepted manuscript (https://doi.org/10.17615/mmbd-f786).
- Gollwitzer, P. M., & Sheeran, P. (2006). Implementation intentions and goal achievement: A meta-analysis of effects and processes. *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology*, 38, 69–119. https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1 Supports: the definition. An implementation intention spells out in advance when, where, and how a person will act ("If situation Y is encountered, then I will do X"), so that meeting the situation prompts the action. Abstract only.
- Milkman, K. L., Beshears, J., Choi, J. J., Laibson, D., & Madrian, B. C. (2011). Using implementation intentions prompts to enhance influenza vaccination rates. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 108(26), 10415–10420. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1103170108 Supports: asking for more than a date. Abstract only.
- Abel, M., Burger, R., Carranza, E., & Piraino, P. (2019). Bridging the intention-behavior gap? The effect of plan-making prompts on job search and employment. *American Economic Journal: Applied Economics*, 11(2), 284–301. https://doi.org/10.1257/app.20170566 Supports: use outside wealthy Western countries. Abstract; the country and sample size are from the working-paper version.
- The Behavioural Insights Team (2024). *EAST: Four simple ways to apply behavioural insights* (revised and updated edition). https://bi.team/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/BIT-EAST.pdf Supports: the technique as a named step. "Help people plan their response to events" is one of three parts of "Make it timely".
- Datta, S., & Mullainathan, S. (2012). *Behavioral Design: A New Approach to Development Policy*. CGD Policy Paper 016. https://www.cgdev.org/sites/default/files/Datta_Mullainathan_Behavioral_Design.pdf Supports: the technique as a named step. Its fifth design principle is "Reduce inattention: reminders and implementation intentions".

None found: a review that compares effects by country or income level, or a field trial of a plan prompt for a one-off, dated action in Asia or Latin America. Searches covered reviews of implementation intentions, differences by culture, publication bias, and field trials of planning prompts in low- and middle-income countries.

What depends on local conditions is whether the person controls the plan: who decides when they may travel, whether the place is open when they are free, and whether saying or writing a plan in front of others is safe.

## Replication

The direction replicates across hundreds of tests, most of them small (a median of about 30 people per group). Field trials find effects of a few percentage points or none. Results for repeated behaviors are weakest: none for gym visits, little for water chlorination, and a small average effect for physical activity in the meta-analysis. The same meta-analysis found no reliable effect on vaccination rates or on voting, although single trials of each have found one. Where the comparison group also received a clear explanation of what to do, as in the South African self-test trial, the prompt added nothing.

## What to check locally

1. Whether the intention exists, asked about a specific day and not "someday".
2. Whether the time and place are the person's to choose: permission, transport, shifts.
3. Whether the place is open and stocked at the hours people are free.
4. Whether the person can say the plan in their own language to whoever delivers the prompt.
5. Who sees or hears the plan, above all where the action is sensitive.
