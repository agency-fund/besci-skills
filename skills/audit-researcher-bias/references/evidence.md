# Evidence: Check your team's assumptions about the audience

## Sources

- World Bank (2015). *World Development Report 2015: Mind, Society, and Behavior*, chapter 10, "The biases of development professionals", pp. 180–191. https://doi.org/10.1596/978-1-4648-0342-0 Supports: the premise that professionals misjudge the people they serve, and the check for assumed difference. The report's team surveyed World Bank staff and set their predictions against answers from poor respondents. It found that staff "imagine that poor individuals are very different from themselves in their self-perceptions, but in fact they are not", and that staff read identical data less accurately when it was presented as evidence on minimum wage laws than as evidence on a skin cream. Among its remedies is subjecting "the key assumptions and arguments underlying policies to a critical and adversarial process". Read in the full text: the opening, the sections on confirmation bias and on mental models, box 10.3, and the conclusion.
- Gyani, A., Tindall, K., & Gallagher, R. (2022). *Explore Field Guide*. The Behavioural Insights Team. https://www.bi.team/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/BIT-Explore-Field-Guide.pdf Supports: writing beliefs down before research. Its first planning step, "Understand what you already 'know'", says: "You will start your Explore project with some preconceptions. It is important to document these", so that the team can "critically evaluate these later". That step was read in full.
- Jang, C., Koki, E., Nyaga, R., Okafor, A., Singh, J., Vang, A., & Wendel, S. (2024). *The Busara toolkit: leveraging behavioral science for development*. Busara Groundwork No. 10. https://doi.org/10.62372/WQSB6195 Supports: prompt b and the carry-over check. Its step "Catalog your perspective" asks what insider and outsider knowledge the team has from its background and lived experience, and then: "What are your information gaps? Preconceived notions? Biases and beliefs?" That step was read in full.
- Lilienfeld, S. O., Ammirati, R., & Landfield, K. (2009). Giving debiasing away: Can psychological research on correcting cognitive errors promote human welfare? *Perspectives on Psychological Science*, 4(4), 390–398. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-6924.2009.01144.x Supports: the limit on what the audit claims. The authors review "the knowns and unknowns of debiasing techniques against confirmation bias" and the "potential barriers to their real-world efficacy". Abstract only.

The three prompts, the four checks, and the rule that the audit is repeated are this skill's own and come from no published source.

## Where it has been tested

Searched November 2026. The skill is a procedure, so this table records where its sources come from.

| Source | Type | Countries | Finding |
|---|---|---|---|

Staff in country offices answered no differently from staff at headquarters |

The procedure does not depend on the audience. The answers do: every "check locally" line is a question that only people from or close to the audience can answer.

## Replication

Not applicable. None of the sources tests whether a team that writes its beliefs down before research reaches different conclusions from one that does not.

## What to check locally

1. Each fact named under "Check locally" in the report, asked of members of the audience and not of staff.
2. Whether someone from or close to the audience has read the flags.
3. Whether the beliefs flagged at kickoff are still held after the first field findings.
