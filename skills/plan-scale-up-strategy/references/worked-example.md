# Worked example: an antenatal iron-folate adherence package moving from four districts to national delivery in Malawi

A full run of the skill on a realistic case, showing the output it should produce. The case is illustrative, not a real program.

## The request

"We piloted the Chitetezo antenatal iron-folate adherence package in 4 districts of Malawi. At the first antenatal visit a Health Surveillance Assistant (HSA) gives a 15-minute counseling session with a picture card; at 4 weeks the HSA makes one home visit with a pill count and leaves a reminder calendar. Our NGO trained and supervised the HSAs together with the district health offices. Our scalability assessment says 'promising, test first': (1) HSA counseling without NGO supervision still raises adherence, to be tested in 2 new districts over 6 months; (2) the picture cards can be printed and distributed through the government supply chain. The Ministry of Health's Reproductive Health Directorate (RHD) has asked in writing for national roll-out through the HSA cadre across 28 districts. The national budget year starts in July; our donor funds us for 18 more months. HSAs report to district health offices, and HSA supervisors cover 25 to 40 HSAs each. The RHD director is the champion; the nutrition unit is wary because it runs its own iron-folate supply program. National elections are in 2 years, and a malaria campaign will use HSAs in months 9 to 11. Nobody outside the pilot districts uses the card yet. Please write the scale-up strategy."

## The output

```markdown
# Scale-up strategy: Chitetezo antenatal iron-folate adherence package, Malawi

**Scalability judgment carried over:** promising, test first; open tests: (1) HSA counseling without NGO supervision still raises adherence, due month 8; (2) cards printed and shipped through the government supply chain, due month 5
**Type of scaling:** vertical first, then horizontal in waves, because HSAs will deliver routinely only once the package is in the antenatal guideline, the HSA job description, and the July budget, and because wave 2 cannot start before test (1) reports

## 1. The innovation
| Part | Hardware or software | Essential? | Judgment tested? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15-minute counseling with a picture card at the first visit | software, with the card as hardware | essential | yes: the pilot's main comparison |
| Home visit at 4 weeks with a pill count | software | essential | no: the pilot never ran without it |
| Reminder calendar | hardware | could be simplified | no |
| Monthly observation of each HSA by NGO staff | software | essential in some form | no: test (1) checks whether district supervision can replace it |
| NGO data clerks entering visit records | software | not essential | no: replaced by two lines on the HSA monthly report |

**Still appropriate to proceed?** Yes. The package uses an existing cadre and adds about 12 tasks per HSA a month.

## 2. The user organization
- **Who adopts, who delivers:** the RHD adopts and sets the guideline; district health offices control HSA time; HSAs deliver, observed by HSA supervisors
- **Perceived need and champion:** yes; the RHD director asked in writing for 28 districts
- **Capacity gaps:** supervisors covering 40 HSAs cannot observe each one monthly; districts above 1 to 30 move to quarterly observation, on a schedule the RHD writes by month 4
- **Timing:** the antenatal guideline is under revision this year

## 3. The environment
| Factor | Helps or hinders | Action | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Policy and politics | Helps: guideline under revision. Hinders: elections in 2 years may change ministry leadership | Insert the package as a guideline annex by month 6, before the election year | RHD director |
| Bureaucracy | Hinders: a budget line missed by April waits a year | Submit the card print run and training line by April | RHD planning officer |
| Other sectors and programs | Hinders: the nutrition unit is wary; the malaria campaign uses HSAs in months 9 to 11 | Joint meeting by month 2, offering the pill count as a shared indicator; schedule wave 2 training outside the campaign | RHD director; NGO training lead |
| Social and economic context | Not assessed beyond the pilot districts | Ask district officers in wave 2 about travel distances for home visits | NGO training lead |
| People's needs and rights | Home visits enter women's homes | Keep consent to the visit in the counseling script | RHD antenatal program officer |

## 4. The resource team
- **Members:** the NGO training lead and two supervision officers, including from the user organization: the RHD antenatal program officer
- **Skills to add:** budget advocacy; the RHD planning officer joins the team
- **Role ends:** month 16, two months before funding ends; from wave 3 the RHD's regional trainers train

## 5. Vertical scaling: changes needed
| Change | Needed? | Action | Lead |
|---|---|---|---|
| Policy | no | none | none |
| Political commitment | yes | Brief the Secretary for Health before the election year | RHD director |
| Law | no | Confirm with the ministry's legal office that HSA pill counts need no authorization | NGO country lead |
| Regulations and guidelines | yes | Package added as an annex to the antenatal guideline | RHD director |
| Budget | yes | Print run and training line in the July budget | RHD planning officer |
| Logistics | yes | Cards and calendars through the government supply chain (test 2) | RHD logistics officer |
| Information systems | yes | Two lines on the HSA monthly report: counseling sessions, home visits with pill counts | RHD monitoring officer |
| Supervision | yes | One-page observation checklist for HSA supervisors | NGO supervision officers, handed to districts |
| Staff appraisal and incentives | unknown | Ask whether HSA appraisal counts the new tasks | RHD human resources officer |
| Training curricula | yes | One day for HSAs, half a day for supervisors, in the HSA in-service curriculum | NGO training lead, then RHD regional trainers |
| Workforce | no | none | none |
| Information materials | yes | Picture card and calendar | RHD logistics officer |

## 6. Horizontal scaling
- **Waves:** wave 1, months 1 to 8: the 2 test districts and the 4 pilot districts under district supervision; wave 2, months 12 to 16: 10 districts, waiting on test (1); wave 3, months 17 to 24: the remaining 12, trained by RHD regional trainers
- **Site differences and adaptations:** card language in the northern region; home-visit day where HSAs cover long distances
- **Cost per wave and who pays:** wave 1 on NGO funds; wave 2 split between NGO funds and the July budget line; wave 3 on the government line; the RHD planning officer costs each wave by month 3
- **Monitoring outcomes:** counseling sessions and home visits from the HSA monthly report; quarterly supervisor observations; adherence by pill count in a sample of women each wave

## 7. Diversification
None until wave 3 is routine. The nutrition unit's request to add a dietary module is logged for review at month 24.

## 8. Spontaneous scaling
Not known to occur. If districts outside the waves start using the card, give them the checklist and add them to monitoring.

## 9. Recommended actions
| Category | Recommendation | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence | Run test (1) in 2 new districts | high |
| Institutionalize | Guideline annex; July budget line; two lines on the HSA report | high |
| User organization | Quarterly observation schedule for districts above 1 to 30 | high |
| Environment | Joint meeting with the nutrition unit | medium |
| Resource team | RHD planning officer joins; hand supervision to districts by month 16 | medium |
| Diversification | Review the dietary module at month 24 | low |

## Operational plan
| Action | Owner (role, organization) | By when | Depends on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Start test (1) in 2 new districts | NGO training lead | month 1 | none |
| Joint meeting with the nutrition unit | RHD director | month 2 | none |
| Cost each wave | RHD planning officer | month 3 | none |
| Submit the print-run and training line for the July budget | RHD planning officer | month 4 (April) | none |
| Ship cards to wave-1 districts through the supply chain | RHD logistics officer | month 5 | test (2) |
| Guideline annex approved | RHD director | month 6 | none |
| Wave 2 training in 10 districts | NGO training lead with RHD regional trainers | month 12 | test (1) positive by month 8; after the malaria campaign |
| Hand supervision to district offices | NGO supervision officers | month 16 | wave 2 complete |

**Review date:** month 8, against the result of test (1) and the first supervisor observations.

## What to check before relying on this
- Whether the RHD's written request binds district health officers, who control HSA time; if districts can decline, wave 2 needs district-level requests.
- Test (1): if counseling without NGO supervision does not raise adherence, wave 2 does not start and the scalability judgment is redone.
```
