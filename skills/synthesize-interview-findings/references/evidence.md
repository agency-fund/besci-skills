# Evidence: Turn interview transcripts into a barrier diagnosis

## Sources

- Gale, N. K., Heath, G., Cameron, E., Rashid, S., & Redwood, S. (2013). Using the framework method for the analysis of qualitative data in multi-disciplinary health research. *BMC Medical Research Methodology*, 13, 117. https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2288-13-117 Supports: the seven stages of the Framework Method (transcription, familiarization, coding, developing a working analytical framework, applying it, charting data into the matrix, interpreting); the matrix of cases in rows by codes in columns; charts that "include references to interesting or illustrative quotations"; doing "some open coding on at least a few of the transcripts" even in a broadly deductive study; at least two researchers coding the first few transcripts independently; and the warning that a statement such as "13 out of 20 participants said X" is meaningless "because the sampling in qualitative research is not designed to be representative of a wider population". The authors add that an experienced qualitative researcher should lead the analysis. Full text.
- Atkins, L., Francis, J., Islam, R., et al. (2017). A guide to using the Theoretical Domains Framework of behaviour change to investigate implementation problems. *Implementation Science*, 12, 77. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13012-017-0605-9 Supports: deductive coding of interview text to domains; the three criteria for judging which domains matter, "(1) relatively high frequency of specific beliefs and/or themes (not relevant to focus group interviews); (2) presence of conflicting beliefs; and (3) evidence of strong beliefs that may affect the target behaviour"; noting responses that fit no domain, most often because they are not about the target behavior; and checking agreement between two coders. Full text.
- UNICEF (2022). *Social & Behaviour Change at UNICEF: Programme Guidance*. https://www.sbcguidance.org/ Supports: the steps of diagnosing the situation (organize the research, identify themes, develop insights, write problem and opportunity statements); "Get more than one person to read each transcript"; "Don't neglect outliers", since viable local solutions are often hidden in them; triangulating each insight against other evidence; and the "3x rule" of three days of analysis for each day of fieldwork.
- Jang, C., Koki, E., Nyaga, R., Okafor, A., Singh, J., Vang, A., & Wendel, S. (2024). *The Busara toolkit: leveraging behavioral science for development*. Busara Groundwork No. 10. https://doi.org/10.62372/WQSB6195 Supports: tying findings to the research questions first and giving other results "second priority"; transcription by "a trained person from the local context"; and judging qualitative findings on transferability, not on generalizability to a population.
- Paul, E., & David, T. (n.d.). *Integrating qualitative methods into randomized evaluations*. J-PAL Research Resources. https://www.povertyactionlab.org/resource/integrating-qualitative-methods-randomized-evaluations Supports: triangulating findings with other sources of data, and the caution that small qualitative samples raise concerns about representativeness.

The procedure does not depend on the population. The reading does: whether a summary keeps the respondent's meaning depends on whether the reader knows the language and the place.

## Replication

Not applicable. No source tests whether two analysts reach the same verdicts from the same interviews. The TDF guide suggests measuring agreement between two coders on the domain codes, which is a check on coding, not on verdicts.

## What to check locally

1. Whether anyone reading the interviews speaks the language they were held in, and which cells rest on notes or translation.
2. How respondents were chosen, so that a difference between groups means something.
3. Whether the guide asked about every hypothesis, so "not asked" can be told from "asked, said nothing".
4. Whether any interview disclosed harm, and whether the safeguarding response has happened.
