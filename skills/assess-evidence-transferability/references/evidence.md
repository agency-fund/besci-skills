# Evidence: Check whether a study's finding applies in your setting

## Sources

- Bates, M. A., & Glennerster, R. (2017). The generalizability puzzle. *Stanford Social Innovation Review*, 15(3), 50–54. https://doi.org/10.48558/EYY5-3S89 Supports: the four sections of the assessment. The article's four steps are: "What is the disaggregated theory behind the program?"; "Do the local conditions hold for that theory to apply?"; "How strong is the evidence for the required general behavioral change?"; "What is the evidence that the implementation process can be carried out well?" The authors note that not every case needs every step.
- Vivalt, E. (2020). How much can we generalize from impact evaluations? *Journal of the European Economic Association*, 18(6), 3045–3089. https://doi.org/10.1093/jeea/jvaa019 Supports: section 4, on who delivers the intervention, and the expectation that effects differ across settings.
- DellaVigna, S., & Linos, E. (2022). RCTs to scale: Comprehensive evidence from two nudge units. *Econometrica*, 90(1), 81–116. https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA18709 Supports: the rule against carrying a published effect size into a plan.
- Henrich, J., Heine, S. J., & Norenzayan, A. (2010). The weirdest people in the world? *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 33(2–3), 61–83. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X0999152X Supports: treating who was studied as a condition of the result.

Whether a finding travels depends on whether the reason it worked is present in the new place. That reason, not the country, is what the assessment compares.

One review bears directly on this skill and could not be read: Szaszi, B., Goldstein, D. G., Soman, D., & Michie, S. (2025). Generalizability of choice architecture interventions. *Nature Reviews Psychology*, 4(8), 518–529. https://doi.org/10.1038/s44159-025-00471-9 The citation is confirmed; the text is behind a login.

## Replication

Not applicable. The assessment is a structured judgment. None of the sources tests whether two teams reach the same verdict on the same study.

## What to check locally

1. Whether the reason the study gives for its effect is present in the population: ask people, do not assume.
2. Whether the institutions the intervention ran through exist and are trusted: payroll, clinics, schools, mobile money.
3. Whether the outcome the team cares about is the one the study measured.
4. Whether the organization that would deliver it has the capacity the study's implementer had.
