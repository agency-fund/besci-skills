# Evidence: Monitor whether a program is delivered as designed

## Sources

The task is a named step in these guides:

- Skivington, K., Matthews, L., Simpson, S. A., Craig, P., Baird, J., Blazeby, J. M., et al. (2021). A new framework for developing and evaluating complex interventions: update of Medical Research Council guidance. *BMJ*, 374, n2061. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.n2061 Supports: the task. Process evaluation "can answer questions around fidelity and quality of implementation (eg, what is implemented and how?)". Flexibility in delivery may help a program travel, "provided that the key functions of the programme are maintained, and that the adaptations made are clearly understood". Read in full.
- Haynes, L., Service, O., Goldacre, B., & Torgerson, D. (2012). *Test, Learn, Adapt: Developing Public Policy with Randomised Controlled Trials*. Cabinet Office Behavioural Insights Team. https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/test-learn-adapt-developing-public-policy-with-randomised-controlled-trials Supports: the task. Step 6: "One important consideration at this stage is to have a system in place for monitoring the intervention, to ensure that it is being introduced in the way that was originally intended." Step 6 read.
- OECD (2019). *Tools and Ethics for Applied Behavioural Insights: The BASIC Toolkit*. https://doi.org/10.1787/9ea76a8f-en Supports: watching the features that look unimportant. "Avoid diluting behavioural policies by carefully monitoring implementation." Dilution happens because features of a behavioral design "might not seem important" to the people running it; a distributor cancelled the pink paper used for a letter "as it seemed unimportant", and staff may decide "that it is not necessary to follow the procedures devised as part of a behavioural intervention". The section on implementation read.
- The TOPS Program (2017). *Designing for Behavior Change: A Practical Field Guide*. https://web.archive.org/web/20180329030310/http://fsnnetwork.org/sites/default/files/designing_for_behavior_change_a_practical_field_guide.pdf Supports: using records that already exist, checking parts at different frequencies, and how to read the results. Step 10 asks designers to "use methods of measurement that already exist"; "Not all Behaviors, Activities, outcomes and other aspects of a Behavior Change strategy need to be monitored at the same frequency"; and if the audience is reached and quality activities are delivered "but the Behavior is not changing, program planners should first reassess the appropriateness of the Activities selected". Full text.
- Gibson, M. (2021). *Implementation monitoring*. J-PAL Research Resources. https://www.povertyactionlab.org/resource/implementation-monitoring Supports: the data sources and what an evaluation needs caught. "Implementation requires careful monitoring to ensure that the program maintains fidelity to its design." The page names administrative data, site visits ("most useful when research staff show up unannounced"), and back-check surveys; it covers compliance, spillovers, and contamination; and it gives IPA's rule that monitoring data "should be credible, actionable, responsible, and transportable". Full page, updated February 2021.

The method:

- Carroll, C., Patterson, M., Wood, S., Booth, A., Rick, J., & Balain, S. (2007). A conceptual framework for implementation fidelity. *Implementation Science*, 2, 40. https://doi.org/10.1186/1748-5908-2-40 Supports: the dimensions and the essential parts. "Adherence is essentially the bottom-line measurement of implementation fidelity", and it "includes the subcategories of content, frequency, duration and coverage". Its moderators are "intervention complexity, facilitation strategies, quality of delivery, and participant responsiveness". Identifying the essential components "also provides scope for identifying adaptability to local conditions"; "if essential components of an intervention are not known, then fidelity to the whole intervention is needed". The abstract says the framework "requires testing by empirical research". Full text.
- Moore, G. F., Audrey, S., Barker, M., Bond, L., Bonell, C., Hardeman, W., Moore, L., O'Cathain, A., Tinati, T., Wight, D., & Baird, J. (2015). Process evaluation of complex interventions: Medical Research Council guidance. *BMJ*, 350, h1258. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.h1258 Supports: what to capture and the feedback decision. Implementation covers fidelity ("whether the intervention was delivered as intended"), dose ("the quantity of intervention implemented"), adaptations, and reach. Evaluators must "maintain sufficient independence to observe the work of stakeholders critically", and must choose whether to "act as passive observers who feed findings back at the end" or "help to correct problems in implementation as and when they appear"; in effectiveness evaluations researchers "will ideally not engage in continuous quality improvement activities". The guidance treats the line between adaptation and loss of fidelity as unresolved. Full text.
- Bellg, A. J., Borrelli, B., Resnick, B., Hecht, J., Minicucci, D. S., Ory, M., Ogedegbe, G., Orwig, D., Ernst, D., & Czajkowski, S. (2004). Enhancing treatment fidelity in health behavior change studies: Best practices and recommendations from the NIH Behavior Change Consortium. *Health Psychology*, 23(5), 443–451. https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-6133.23.5.443 Supports: checking what participants received and do, as well as what was delivered. Its recommendations "cover study design, provider training, treatment delivery, treatment receipt, and enactment of treatment skills". Abstract only.

Abstract only.

Three rules are this skill's own and come from no source: separate indicators for each dimension of each essential part; a written commitment that the data stay out of appraisal, pay, and discipline; and an owner for every threshold.

## Where it has been tested

Searched November 2026. The skill is a planning procedure and makes no claim about how people behave, so this table records where its sources come from and what studies of the monitoring methods found.

The procedure does not depend on the population. The data sources do: whether registers are filled in during the visit, whether participants can be reached by phone, and whether an unannounced visit reads as support or as audit all vary by place.

## Replication

Not applicable; this is method guidance. The findings on monitoring methods each rest on one study: providers rating themselves higher than observers (one US school program), and participants overstating what a visit covered (one Nigerian study). The MRC guidance treats how far delivery may adapt before fidelity is lost as unresolved.

## What to check locally

1. Whether registers and logs are filled in during the visit or reconstructed later, and by whom.
2. Whether unannounced observation is acceptable to deliverers and the community, and what "monitoring" has meant there before.
3. Whether each deliverer has a supervisor who can act on a threshold within the month.
4. Whether a random sample of participants can be re-contacted: phones, consent, cost.
5. Whether the implementing organization will put the data-use rule in writing.
