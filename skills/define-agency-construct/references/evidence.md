No study was found that tests this framework as a whole, or that compares teams who defined agency this way with teams who did not. On agency with respect to AI tools, the scan found one field experiment outside wealthy Western countries. What depends on the place is what counts as acting on one's own goals: who is expected to decide, whether a shared decision is a constraint or a choice, and whether the words in a borrowed scale mean the same thing.

## Replication

Contested. The theories are long-standing and widely used, but reviews find no agreed definition or standard measure of agency. Where the same scale has been taken to several countries, one study found it comparable and another did not. Spouses in the same household give different answers about who decides. Evidence on agency with respect to AI tools comes from a single field experiment, one survey, and a preprint.

## What to check locally

1. Whether users themselves describe agency the way the team does. Ask a few of them what a person "in charge of" the goal does and says.
2. Who is expected to decide in the household or group, and whether the user wants a say in the decisions the definition names.
3. Whether the words for confidence, control, and choice carry over into the users' language before any construct is fixed.
4. Whether users can act without the tool: on a shared phone, with no connection, or when the service is down.
