# Matching a team's meaning to theory

Use this in steps 4 to 8 to turn what a team says about agency into named constructs and to place them on the 2x2 agency framework. The frameworks are lenses for the team's own meaning, not a menu; borrow from more than one where that fits better. Full citations are in `evidence.md`.

## The 2x2 agency framework

Agency is "a person's capability to navigate toward the future they want" (Wu, Goldberg & Xu, 2026). It has no single fixed definition. Two distinctions give it structure:

- **Forms of agency:** agency **with respect to the end goal** (can users make progress toward outcomes they value, such as health, education, and livelihoods?) and agency **with respect to the digital tool** (can users understand, question, adapt, control, and use the tool in pursuit of their goals?).
- **Manifestations of agency:** **internally experienced** agency (thoughts and feelings) and **externally expressed** agency (choices and actions).

| | With respect to the end goal | With respect to the digital tool |
|---|---|---|
| **Internally experienced** (thoughts and feelings) | Self-efficacy, perceived control, hope, goals of one's own | Felt control over the tool, confidence in judging its output, trust that tracks its reliability |
| **Externally expressed** (choices and actions) | Setting, revising, and acting on goals; decisions; persistence | Questioning, adapting, or rejecting advice; choosing when to use it; acting without it |

The grid helps a team see what its definition covers. It is not a list every definition must fill.

When a team's meaning involves decisions made with a spouse, parents, or community, a shared decision is not less agency by default. Being the decision-maker is a valid sign of agency only if the person wants to be involved in that decision (Donald & Hernandez-de-Benito, 2021). Ask that, and name the construct in those terms.

## The frameworks

### Bandura: agency as a set of processes (psychology)
Social cognitive theory describes agency through intentionality and forethought, self-regulation by self-reactive influence, and self-reflectiveness about one's capabilities. It names three modes: direct personal agency, proxy agency "that relies on others to act on one's behest", and collective agency (Bandura, 2001). Self-efficacy, a person's confidence that they can do a given thing, is a construct from this theory.
- **Fits when** the team's words are about confidence or capability, or when plans and revisions can be observed in conversation logs.
- **For AI tools,** proxy agency is the key idea: relying on a tool can be agency exercised through a proxy, not only agency lost.

### Sen and the capability approach (economics and development)
In Sen's account, as Alkire (2008) reads it, agency is exercised with respect to goals the person values, and it includes effective power as well as direct control. Alkire observes that the measures used in development tend to capture control and not effective freedom.
- **Fits when** norms, family, institutions, or money decide whether agency can be used, and when using a tool may not turn into anything that matters in users' lives.

### Kabeer: resources, agency, achievements (gender and development)
Empowerment is the process by which people who have been denied the ability to make strategic life choices acquire it. That ability has three dimensions, resources, agency, and achievements, which are "indivisible in determining the meaning of an indicator" (Kabeer, 1999). Access to a tool is a resource; agency is what users do with it; achievements are the outcomes.
- **Fits when** a team says "empowerment" and needs to say which part it means.

### Donald et al.: goal setting, sense of agency, acting on goals (women's agency)
Three dimensions: goal setting, "perceived control and ability to initiate action toward goals" (sense of agency), and acting on goals (Donald et al., 2020).
- **Fits when** users face constraints set by others, especially women and girls in low- and middle-income settings.

### Self-determination theory: autonomy as a need
Competence, autonomy, and relatedness are psychological needs; when they are met, motivation and well-being improve (Ryan & Deci, 2000). People's reasons for acting run along a continuum of autonomy from external pressure to reasons they hold as their own (Ryan & Connell, 1989). The Relative Autonomy Index built on this measures how far a person acts under coercion or social pressure, and has been used with women in Chad (Vaz, Pratley & Alkire, 2016).
- **Fits when** the team needs to tell reliance a user endorses from compliance or drift.
- **Check the wording with users.** An adapted scale of need satisfaction measured equivalently among young adults in Belgium, China, the United States, and Peru (Chen et al., 2015); that says nothing yet about your users.

### Jigsaw: five dimensions AI can strengthen or weaken (human-AI interaction)
Jigsaw's ethnographic research names five dimensions of agency that AI can strengthen or weaken: instrumental, cognitive, affective, social, and structural (Wu, Goldberg & Xu, 2026). A preprint from an overlapping team reports that daily chatbot users link sustained use to gains in agency they perceive, and cautions that such gains "may not necessarily translate into material effects, structural empowerment, or long-term capacity" (Beacock et al., 2026).
- **Fits when** naming which kind of agency a specific AI feature helps or erodes.
- **Caution:** 51 daily chatbot users in the United States, Germany, and Singapore; not peer reviewed.

### Research on people and automation: agency with respect to the tool
A review of 30 years of human-computer interaction research finds that "agency" and "autonomy" are used for a wide range of things, from sense of control to material independence to identity, and calls for more specific use (Bennett et al., 2023). A tool can offer high automation and high human control at once (Shneiderman, 2020). The target is appropriate reliance: trust guides how far people rely on automation, and that trust can be more or less appropriate (Lee & See, 2004). Both errors are possible: over-reliance on automation, and neglect of automation that works (Parasuraman & Riley, 1997).

### The Agency Fund's program lens
Programs build agency when they nurture people's capacity to form goals, equip them with strategies for complex decisions, and support their resilience after setbacks (Walsh & Madon, 2024). These map onto goal setting, decision-making, and persistence.

## What erosion looks like, by cell

| | With respect to the end goal | With respect to the digital tool |
|---|---|---|
| **Internally experienced** | Confidence rises while capability does not; goals narrow to what the tool suggests | High confidence in the tool with little checking of its output (Lee et al., 2025); feeling unable to question it |
| **Externally expressed** | Performance with the tool goes up while performance without it goes down (Bastani et al., 2025); goals dropped when the tool is unavailable | Asking for the answer in place of working the problem; accepting wrong advice; handing over decisions the user would rather keep |

## When nothing fits

There is no agreed definition or standard measure: a review found many different instruments across 34 studies (Cavazzoni, Fiorini & Veronese, 2022). If the team's meaning has no good match, keep their wording as a local construct. Where a search tool is available, look for a framework built for this population: "Theories and frameworks of [the team's meaning] for [population] in [domain]. For each: what it foregrounds, what it can and cannot detect, and its main critiques. Cite sources."
