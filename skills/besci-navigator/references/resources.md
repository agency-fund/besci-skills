# Resources beyond the library

Guides and method references the navigator can point a person to when a step needs more than a skill, or when the library has no skill for it. The citations are the ones in the repository's `docs/canonical-sources.md`, where each was checked against the publisher's page or the DOI record. Point to a source by its title and link, and say in a clause why it fits the person's step.

Anything not on this page has to be opened before it is named. A title recalled from memory may not exist.

- Jang, C., Koki, E., Nyaga, R., Okafor, A., Singh, J., Vang, A., & Wendel, S. (2024). *The Busara toolkit: leveraging behavioral science for development*. Busara Groundwork No. 10. https://doi.org/10.62372/WQSB6195

## People

- Find My BeSci, https://findmybesci.com, matches organizations with behavioral-science practitioners.
- A local ethics board or institutional review board, for any study that collects data from people. The library's skills do not stand in for one.

## The library itself

- The catalog, the map of how skills chain, and the workflows: https://besci-skills.vercel.app/
- "Propose a skill", for a step the library does not cover: https://besci-skills.vercel.app/propose/

This list is thin on analyzing results. Only the J-PAL resources and the Medical Research Council guidance say much about it, so for a statistical question say that the library's sources do not go far and suggest a statistician.
