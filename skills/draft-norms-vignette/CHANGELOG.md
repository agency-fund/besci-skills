# Changelog

## 0.3.0 - 2026-11-11
Follows the conversational-voice standard (ADR 0009). The vignette opens with a few plain sentences to the user (what respondents predict, what the versions change, and that the answers are predictions about a made-up person); the title becomes "Survey story (vignette)", "Reference group" becomes "Whose behavior and approval the story names", "Respondents and mode" becomes "Respondents and how they are surveyed", "Cues" is glossed as the sentences that change, "Reading" becomes "What each result would mean", and "What to probe when testing the story" becomes "What to ask when trying out the story". The output ends with next moves and their reasons, chosen from candidates listed in the closing, and the skill carries the shared voice block. The method is unchanged. Edited with Claude.

## 0.2.0 - 2026-11-11
Rewritten to the November 2026 standard, at about half its earlier length. The design now follows Bicchieri, Lindemans, and Jiang (2014), read in full: an open question on what might lead the fictional person to act (the earlier draft forbade asking why), the respondent's own view asked after the story, and a story set outside the respondents' own community. The UNICEF and Penn manual and the Social Norms Exploration Tool are now cited from their text, and field studies from Mali, Benin, Rwanda, Ghana, and Zambia were added. Rewritten with Claude.
