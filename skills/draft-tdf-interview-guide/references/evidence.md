# Evidence: Write an interview guide that tests barrier hypotheses

## Sources

- Atkins, L., Francis, J., Islam, R., O'Connor, D., Patey, A., Ivers, N., Foy, R., Duncan, E. M., Colquhoun, H., Grimshaw, J. M., Lawton, R., & Michie, S. (2017). A guide to using the Theoretical Domains Framework of behaviour change to investigate implementation problems. *Implementation Science*, 12, 77. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13012-017-0605-9 Supports: the procedure. The guide says an interview schedule "typically consists of an open question for each theoretical domain to elicit the first response, followed by a series of follow-up prompts", that each question is about the target behavior, that the order should follow the conversation, and that schedules should be piloted. It also warns that "if questions are not asked, the text cannot be coded", which is why every domain gets at least one question. It reports that one-to-one interviews typically last 25 to 45 minutes. The section on interview schedules was read in the full text.
- Cane, J., O'Connor, D., & Michie, S. (2012). Validation of the theoretical domains framework for use in behaviour change and implementation research. *Implementation Science*, 7, 37. https://doi.org/10.1186/1748-5908-7-37 Supports: the 14 domains, and the mapping of domains to COM-B components in the output template (the paper's table 3, on which three experts agreed fully). The method, results, and table 3 were read in the full text.
- McGowan, L. J., Powell, R., & French, D. P. (2020). How can use of the Theoretical Domains Framework be optimized in qualitative research? A rapid systematic review. *British Journal of Health Psychology*, 25(3), 677–694. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjhp.12437 Supports: the open question outside the framework, and using the domains as labels and not as the structure of the conversation. Abstract only.
- Huijg, J. M., Gebhardt, W. A., Crone, M. R., Dusseldorp, E., & Presseau, J. (2014). Discriminant content validity of a theoretical domains framework questionnaire for use in implementation research. *Implementation Science*, 9, 11. https://doi.org/10.1186/1748-5908-9-11 Supports: allowing two domain labels on one question, and cutting Reinforcement, Goals, and Behavioral regulation first. Abstract only.
- Gyani, A., Tindall, K., & Gallagher, R. (2022). *Explore Field Guide*. The Behavioural Insights Team. https://www.bi.team/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/BIT-Explore-Field-Guide.pdf Supports: the running order (introduction, warm-up, deep dive, wrap-up, with sensitive questions in the middle); the kinds of question to avoid (leading, containing a social norm, closed, double-barrelled, complex); and probes that ask for clarification, detail, or what changed. The interview section was read in full.
- Presseau, J., McCleary, N., Lorencatto, F., Patey, A. M., Grimshaw, J. M., & Francis, J. J. (2019). Action, actor, context, target, time (AACTT): a framework for specifying behaviour. *Implementation Science*, 14, 102. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13012-019-0951-x Supports: stating the behavior with its context and target as well as its actor, action, and time. Abstract only.

Four rules are this skill's own and come from no source: the hypothesis label on every question, two or three questions for each hypothesis, the reconstruction question, and reporting a domain as "not raised".

## Where it has been tested

Searched November 2026.

| Source | Type | Countries | Finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Cane, O'Connor & Michie (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1186/1748-5908-7-37) | Sorting task with 37 behavior-change experts; full text, in part | Mostly the United Kingdom (16) and the Netherlands (8) | Supported 14 domains. Two domains clustered weakly |
| [Huijg et al. (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1186/1748-5908-9-11) | 19 judges allocated 79 questionnaire items to domains; abstract only | Not stated in the abstract; the questionnaire was in English and Dutch | Items could be told apart for 11 of 14 domains; Reinforcement, Goals, and Behavioral regulation could not |
| [McGowan, Powell & French (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjhp.12437) | Rapid systematic review, 186 qualitative studies; abstract only | Not stated in the abstract | Most studies with patients or the public reported findings confined to the domains. The authors conclude that rigid use may overlook influences |
| [Forbes et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13012-022-01253-0) | Interviews with 45 health workers in nine hospitals; abstract only | Kenya, Nigeria, South Africa | Influences on current practice fell in 12 domains and were consistent across the three countries |
| [Sethu et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0261308) | 35 interviews with parents and carers; abstract only | India | Themes fell in 11 of the 12 domains of the framework's earlier version |
| [Badacho et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1177/20503121241253960) | Scoping review, 41 articles; abstract only | Low- and middle-income countries in Africa | Barriers to diagnosis could be placed in all 14 domains |
| [Norton, Rodriguez & Willems (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12961-019-0463-9) | Survey and 20 interviews with knowledge brokers; abstract only | Respondents from 56 countries | Codes had to be added or adapted, one of them for influences from beyond the organization. The authors report trouble with overlapping constructs |

No review was found of how the framework has been used in low- and middle-income countries. Searches covered scoping and systematic reviews of the framework in those countries, reviews of how it is used in qualitative research, and interview studies in Kenya, Nigeria, India, and Uganda.

These studies show the domains can hold what respondents say; they do not show that nothing was missed, because most analyses only look inside the domains. What depends on the place is who counts under Social influences, what Environmental context and resources contains, and whether Social or professional role and identity means anything to respondents who are not professionals.

## Replication

The 14-domain structure rests on one expert sorting study. A second study found that judges could not separate three of the domains from others. The framework sorts influences on behavior and predicts nothing, so there is no effect to replicate. No study was found that compares a guide built on the framework with a guide built without it.

## What to check locally

1. Whose opinion matters for this behavior: spouse, in-laws, elders, peers, employer, religious leader.
2. Whether "goal", "plan", "intend", and "confident" translate without changing meaning; use concrete substitutes if not.
3. Whether the interviewer's relationship to the respondent (supervisor, mentor, supplier) will shape the answers.
4. Whether the topic needs ethics review before interviews begin.
