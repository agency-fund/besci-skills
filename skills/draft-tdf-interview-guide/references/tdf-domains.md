# The 14 domains: what to ask about

For step 2 of the skill: the domain each hypothesis belongs to, and what a question in that domain asks about.

The domains and their COM-B components are from Cane, O'Connor & Michie (2012), table 3. The "ask about" column is this skill's plain-language gloss, written for respondents who may not be professionals.

| Domain | COM-B component | Ask about |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge | Capability, psychological | What the person knows about the behavior: what it is, how it is done, why it is recommended |
| Skills | Capability, psychological or physical | Whether they have done it, how it went, which part was hard to do |
| Memory, attention and decision processes | Capability, psychological | Whether it comes to mind at the right moment, and how they decide on the day |
| Behavioral regulation | Capability, psychological | Plans, reminders, and routines they use to keep track of it |
| Environmental context and resources | Opportunity, physical | Time, money, materials, distance, services, and what else is going on when the behavior is due |
| Social influences | Opportunity, social | Who around them does it, who approves, who objects, whose permission is needed |
| Social or professional role and identity | Motivation, reflective or automatic | Whether doing it fits who they are: their job, or their place in the family or community |
| Beliefs about capabilities | Motivation, reflective | How sure they are that they could do it, and what would make them less sure |
| Optimism | Motivation, reflective or automatic | Whether they expect things to turn out well if they try |
| Beliefs about consequences | Motivation, reflective | What they expect to happen, good or bad, if they do it and if they do not |
| Intentions | Motivation, reflective | Whether they have decided to do it, and how firmly |
| Goals | Motivation, reflective | Where it ranks among the other things they are trying to get done |
| Reinforcement | Motivation, automatic | What followed the last time they did it: praise, a cost, a result they could see, nothing |
| Emotion | Motivation, automatic | The feelings that come up around it: fear, shame, worry, relief |

Three domains overlap with others in practice: Reinforcement, Goals, and Behavioral regulation. One question may carry two labels.

When respondents are not professionals, ask about role and identity through family and community ("is this something a mother, a farmer, a man your age is expected to do?"), and name the people whose opinion matters (spouse, in-laws, elders, group leader, employer) in place of "colleagues" or "manager".
