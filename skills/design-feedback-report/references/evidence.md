# Evidence: Design a performance feedback report

## Sources

- Kluger, A. N., & DeNisi, A. (1996). The effects of feedback interventions on performance: A historical review, a meta-analysis, and a preliminary feedback intervention theory. *Psychological Bulletin*, 119(2), 254–284. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.119.2.254 Supports: keeping the report on the task, with no label, praise, or blame of the person. Abstract only.
- Ivers, N., Yogasingam, S., Lacroix, M., et al. (2025). Audit and feedback: effects on professional practice. *Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews*, 2025(3), CD000259. https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD000259.pub4 Supports: one metric measured for each person, the comparison rule, and the next action. Abstract only.
- Ashraf, N., Bandiera, O., & Lee, S. S. (2014). Awards unbundled: Evidence from a natural field experiment. *Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization*, 100, 44–63. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jebo.2014.01.001 Supports: showing no rank, and showing people far below the next step only. Abstract only.
- Schultz, P. W., Nolan, J. M., Cialdini, R. B., Goldstein, N. J., & Griskevicius, V. (2007). The constructive, destructive, and reconstructive power of social norms. *Psychological Science*, 18(5), 429–434. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2007.01917.x Supports: giving people at or above the comparison no lower figure, and a sign of approval. Abstract only.
- Allcott, H. (2011). Social norms and energy conservation. *Journal of Public Economics*, 95(9–10), 1082–1095. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpubeco.2011.03.003 Supports: reading results separately for both ends, and not relying on the sign of approval. Abstract only.
- Byrne, D. P., La Nauze, A., & Martin, L. A. (2018). Tell me something I don't already know: Informedness and the impact of information programs. *The Review of Economics and Statistics*, 100(3), 510–527. https://doi.org/10.1162/rest_a_00695 Supports: the same. Households that had overestimated their relative use, and low users, consumed more after personal feedback with peer comparisons. Abstract only.
- Allcott, H., & Rogers, T. (2014). The short-run and long-run effects of behavioral interventions: Experimental evidence from energy conservation. *American Economic Review*, 104(10), 3003–3037. https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.104.10.3003 Supports: saying what is expected when reports stop. Abstract only.

Four rules are this skill's own: "top peers" is set at the top quarter; the peer average is shown only to people below it; people far below see the next step and not the full gap; and the pilot is read separately for both ends.

| [Ashraf, Bandiera & Lee (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jebo.2014.01.001) | Field experiment in a national health worker training program; abstract only | Zambia | Recognition from the employer and visibility to others raised performance. | [Whidden et al. | [Muralidharan & Sundararaman (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0297.2010.02373.x) | Randomized trial in schools; abstract only | India | Diagnostic test results and low-stakes monitoring were given to teachers. Teachers worked harder while observed, and students did no better on independent tests |

The scan found no trial outside wealthy Western countries of what people at the top and bottom of a comparison should be shown. Outside those countries the results differ by audience: reports to households cut water and electricity use in three countries, while results for workers range from more home visits in Mali to lower performance in Zambia and no learning gain in India. What depends on the place is whether people trust the number, whether a peer group is small enough for "the average" to mean named colleagues, and what a supervisor does with the figures.

## Replication

That feedback helps on average has held across two large reviews, with very uneven results: more than a third of feedback interventions in the 1996 meta-analysis reduced performance. That people who are already doing better than the comparison can slip is documented in two energy studies. The remedy is less certain: a sign of approval removed the slip in the 2007 study, played an insignificant role in the much larger US program, and did not prevent it in the 2018 study. Decay after reports stop has been measured in one large US program.

## What to check locally

1. Whether each person's number can be computed the same way every period, and whether the person will believe it.
2. Whether a peer comparison stays private here, or people know who "the average" is.
3. Whether the sign of approval reads as approval and not as sarcasm.
4. Whether a supervisor will see the report, and what they will do with it.
5. What happened in the pilot to people who started above the comparison.
