# Worked example: a sign and a script for a screened deposit spot

A full run of the skill on a realistic case, showing the output it should produce. The case is illustrative, not a real program.

## The request

"We have chosen the intervention: a privacy screen and a marked quick-deposit position at the mobile-money agent's kiosk inside Gikomba and Toi markets, so deposits cannot be seen from neighboring stalls. The barrier, confirmed in vendor interviews, is that counting and depositing cash at closing is seen by neighbors, who then ask to borrow. The behavior: women with produce stalls deposit the day's surplus cash into their mobile-money savings wallet before leaving the market each trading day. Vendors decide at the kiosk at closing time; we watched this on six evenings. They speak Swahili and reading levels are mixed. Our field coordinator is a native Swahili speaker. We have no figures on how many vendors deposit now. Write the sign and what the agent should say."

## The output

```markdown
Here are a sign and one line for the agent, both presenting the screened position as the fast lane, so stepping behind it reads as saving time rather than hiding money. The copy makes no claim about what other vendors do: you have no figures, and such a line would draw eyes to the deposits the screen hides. It is English working copy; your field coordinator writes the Swahili, and eight vendors test that version before it goes up.

# Draft wording: a sign and a script for the screened deposit spot

**Behavior:** Women with produce stalls in Gikomba and Toi markets deposit the day's surplus cash into their mobile-money savings wallet before leaving the market each trading day.
**Barrier it answers:** Counting and depositing cash at the kiosk at closing is seen by neighboring vendors, who then ask to borrow.
**Technique:** A change to the physical setting: a screen and a marked deposit position at the agent's kiosk. Taxonomy label to check: 12.1 Restructuring the physical environment.
**What it must contain:** A change to the place itself that makes the wanted behavior easier to do. Here the content has to make the screened position easy to find and ordinary to use, without drawing attention to who uses it. (paraphrase)
**Channel and moment:** A sign at the screened position, with one spoken line from the agent, at the kiosk at closing time. Confirmed: the team observed vendors deciding there on six evenings. No text message, because the barrier is being seen at the kiosk, not a lack of information.
**Language note:** The copy below is English working copy. The field coordinator writes the Swahili sign and script afresh from it, and the test runs on the Swahili version.

## Draft content
Sign at the screened position (working copy):
> **Quick deposit here.** Behind the screen. No waiting.

Agent, pointing to the position (working copy):
> "Quick deposits this side. It's the fast lane."

## What each part of the draft delivers

| Part | What it delivers |
|---|---|
| "Quick deposit here. Behind the screen." | Makes the changed setting findable: the screen helps only vendors who know where it is and what it is for |
| "No waiting." / "It's the fast lane." | Makes the screened position ordinary to use: choosing it reads as saving time, not as hiding money |
| Agent points and does not name amounts or people | Keeps the deposit out of the neighbors' sight and hearing, which is what the screen is for |

## Delivery notes
- **Sender:** The agent's kiosk. Vendors already hand it their cash. No program logo, which would mark users of the position as "the program's people".
- **Timing:** The sign is permanent. The agent says the line only when a vendor comes to the kiosk with cash at closing.
- **How often is too often:** Once per vendor per visit. Agents do not call vendors over: an agent calling out for deposits brings back the attention the screen removes.

## EAST check: is it easy, attractive, social, and timely?
- **Easy:** One action: step to the marked position. The sign is eight words.
- **Attractive:** "Quick" and "no waiting" answer what vendors want at closing, which is to leave.
- **Social:** None made: the team has no figures on who deposits, and a line such as "most vendors deposit here" would point neighbors at the act the screen hides.
- **Timely:** At the kiosk at closing, where and when the vendor decides, not earlier in the day by phone.

## Test before launch
Show the Swahili sign, with no explanation, to eight vendors from the two markets who have not seen it. Each says what the marked position is for and shows where she would stand. Pass: all eight name depositing or a fast transaction, and none says it is where the program watches deposits. Any such reading sends the copy back for redrafting.

## What this content does not do
- It does not replace the screen. If market management refuses the fixture, there is nothing for the sign to point to, and the choice of intervention is reopened.
- It does not tell vendors how the savings wallet works. If the test shows vendors do not know, that is a different barrier.

## Where you could go from here
That's the draft.
- Have the field coordinator write the Swahili sign and line and run the eight-vendor test: the English is only working copy, and the test counts only on the version vendors will read.
- Plan a short trial at one kiosk with `plan-prototype-test`: fits once the wording passes, if you want to see whether vendors actually step behind the screen at closing, which a comprehension test cannot show.
- Ask market management about the screen now: if they refuse the fixture, the sign has nothing to point to and the choice of intervention is reopened.
I'd lean toward the Swahili draft and the test first, because nothing else about the wording can be judged until that version exists.
Questions you could ask next: "What should the agent say if a neighbor asks what the screen is for?" · "How would we know after a month whether vendors use the screened position?"
```
