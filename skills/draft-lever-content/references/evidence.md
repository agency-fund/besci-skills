# Evidence: Write an intervention's messages and scripts

## Sources

- Kok, G., Gottlieb, N. H., Peters, G.-J. Y., Mullen, P. D., Parcel, G. S., Ruiter, R. A. C., Fernández, M. E., Markham, C., & Bartholomew, L. K. (2016). A taxonomy of behaviour change methods: An Intervention Mapping approach. *Health Psychology Review*, 10(3), 297–312. https://doi.org/10.1080/17437199.2015.1077155 Supports: the trace in step 5. Abstract only. The authors set three conditions for a method to work: it targets a determinant that predicts the behavior, it can change that determinant, and it is "translated into a practical application in a way that preserves the parameters for effectiveness and fits with the target population, culture, and context". They warn that a poor translation can lead people to conclude, wrongly, that the method does not work.
- Fernandez, M. E., Ruiter, R. A. C., Markham, C. M., & Kok, G. (2019). Intervention Mapping: Theory- and evidence-based health promotion program planning: Perspective and examples. *Frontiers in Public Health*, 7, 209. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2019.00209 Supports: drafting and then pretesting (step 8). Read in full. Step 4 of Intervention Mapping drafts messages, materials, and protocols, then pretests them. A pretest typically assesses comprehension, attractiveness, acceptance, believability, and motivation, and comes after the messages are designed and before the materials are final.
- Kettle, S., & Persian, R. (2022). *Target, Explore, Solution, Trial, Scale: An introduction to running simple behavioural insights projects*. The Behavioural Insights Team. https://www.bi.team/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/BIT-Handbook-How-to-run-simple-BI-projects.pdf Supports: the drafting rules in step 4 and the test in step 8. Read in full. Its tips for communications: write so that "a 10-year old should be able to understand it"; keep it short; say why the reader received the message, what to do, and how to do it. Its user testing starts from a rough draft, can use as few as five people when the audience is alike, and asks people to use the draft "instead of just asking them whether they do [understand], to which they might answer 'yes' to please us". It adds that such testing does not show whether the intervention will change the behavior.
- The Behavioural Insights Team (2024). *EAST: Four simple ways to apply behavioural insights* (revised and updated edition). https://bi.team/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/BIT-EAST.pdf Supports: the EAST check in step 7 and the drafting rules in step 4. Section 1.3, "Simplify messages", read. Its five lessons: present the key message early, keep language simple, be specific about the recommended action, give a single point of contact, and remove information not needed to act. It reports that making a request easier to understand often raises response rates, and that "improvements do not come from simplified language alone".
- The TOPS Program (2017). *Designing for Behavior Change: A Practical Field Guide*. Supports: writing messages from the barrier that was found. The passage on messages read. Messages should name the audience, rest on the formative research, be short, use the command form of the verb, and leave out basic information the audience already knows.
- Michie, S., Richardson, M., Johnston, M., Abraham, C., Francis, J., Hardeman, W., Eccles, M. P., Cane, J., & Wood, C. E. (2013). The behavior change technique taxonomy (v1) of 93 hierarchically clustered techniques: Building an international consensus for the reporting of behavior change interventions. *Annals of Behavioral Medicine*, 46(1), 81–95. https://doi.org/10.1007/s12160-013-9486-6 Supports: step 1, as one place a technique's published definition can be found. Abstract only.
- Whittingham, J. R. D., Ruiter, R. A. C., Castermans, D., Huiberts, A., & Kok, G. (2008). Designing effective health education materials: Experimental pre-testing of a theory-based brochure to increase knowledge. *Health Education Research*, 23(3), 414–426. https://doi.org/10.1093/her/cym018 Supports: the warning that a passed comprehension test is not proof of behavior change. Abstract only.

Four rules are this skill's own and come from no source: the trace table, the check of covering the technique's name, the rule that a native speaker writes the final copy afresh, and the frequency ceiling.

Both trials outside wealthy Western countries are of tax messages sent by a government to people who can read a letter or an email. What depends on local conditions is whether the audience can read the copy at all, in which language, through which channel, and whom they trust as the sender.

## Replication

Mixed. That a clearer request gets more response has held in many of one team's trials and in a US field experiment. It is not a rule: in Guatemala two of four redesigned letters raised payment, and the team behind the drafting rules reports that simpler language alone is not enough. Understanding is not action either: the Dutch brochure raised knowledge and nothing else.

## What to check locally

1. Whether the channel reaches the audience: who owns the phone, who can read a sign, who hears the script.
2. Whether people from the audience can say or show what the final-language copy asks of them.
3. Whom the audience trusts as the sender of this content.
4. Whether any figure about what other people do is true, recent, and about this audience.
