# Evidence: Turn a vague goal into one clear behavior

## Sources

- Michie, S., van Stralen, M. M., & West, R. (2011). The behaviour change wheel: A new method for characterising and designing behaviour change interventions. *Implementation Science*, 6, 42. https://doi.org/10.1186/1748-5908-6-42 Supports: starting intervention design from a behavior and what must change for it to occur.
- Michie, S., Atkins, L., & West, R. (2014). *The Behaviour Change Wheel: A Guide to Designing Interventions*. Silverback Publishing. Supports: the first three steps (define the problem in behavioral terms, select the target behavior, specify the target behavior), the four selection criteria, and APEASE. The book is not open. The steps and criteria here were checked against open accounts of it by its authors, including West, R., Michie, S., Atkins, L., Chadwick, P., & Lorencatto, F. (2019), *Achieving behaviour change: A guide for local government and partners* (Public Health England), which rates candidate behaviors with APEASE.
- Kettle, S., & Persian, R. (2022). *Target, Explore, Solution, Trial, Scale: An introduction to running simple behavioural insights projects*. The Behavioural Insights Team. https://www.bi.team/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/BIT-Handbook-How-to-run-simple-BI-projects.pdf Supports: the target statement (whose behavior, what the behavior is, when it should happen); the rule that the target "should always be a behaviour, not an attitude or awareness"; and rating each candidate on impact and feasibility from 1 to 5.
- OECD (2019). *Tools and Ethics for Applied Behavioural Insights: The BASIC Toolkit*. https://doi.org/10.1787/9ea76a8f-en Supports: breaking a policy problem into behaviors, filtering them by priority, and defining each by the "who", the "what", and the "when-and-where".
- Kneebone, S., Smith, L., & Fielding, K. (2017). The Impact-Likelihood Matrix: A policy tool for behaviour prioritisation. *Environmental Science & Policy*, 70, 9–20. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsci.2016.11.013 Supports: scoring impact and likelihood of adoption as separate things.
- Presseau, J., McCleary, N., Lorencatto, F., Patey, A. M., Grimshaw, J. M., & Francis, J. J. (2019). Action, actor, context, target, time (AACTT): a framework for specifying behaviour. *Implementation Science*, 14, 102. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13012-019-0951-x Supports: specifying a behavior by who acts, what they do, and when.

Two rules are this skill's own and come from no source: the basis label on every score, and the requirement that one candidate sit outside what the program has already built.

## Where it has been tested

Searched November 2026. The skill is a prioritization procedure, so this table records where its sources come from.

| Source | Type | Countries | Finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Michie, van Stralen & West (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1186/1748-5908-6-42) | Framework paper; full text | United Kingdom | Independent coders used the framework to classify the parts of existing strategies, and their agreement was measured |
| [Kettle & Persian (2022)](https://www.bi.team/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/BIT-Handbook-How-to-run-simple-BI-projects.pdf) | Practitioner guide; full text | Written by a UK team for project teams in any country | No test of the method is reported |
| [Kneebone, Smith & Fielding (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsci.2016.11.013) | Method paper; citation confirmed, text not read | Not read | Not read |

The procedure does not depend on the population. The scores do: impact and likelihood are local facts, and the skill labels them guesses until someone has data or has asked the audience.

## Replication

Not applicable. None of the sources tests whether two teams give the same candidates the same scores.

## What to check locally

1. How many people already do each candidate behavior, from monitoring data or a short count.
2. Which candidates the audience says it would take up, asked of members of the audience and not of staff.
3. Whether the selected behavior can be observed without a new data system.
